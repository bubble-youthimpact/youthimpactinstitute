Policy Memo 106

Subject
Climate Reparations Begin at Home: A Youth Agenda for Environmental Redress
Date
November 11, 2024

Executive Summary

The Democratic Party’s climate platform has advanced bold goals—but often without addressing the historical injustices that shape current environmental inequality. From redlined neighborhoods with toxic air to tribal lands devastated by extraction, youth of color are calling for climate reparations that go beyond carbon cuts. This memo proposes that the DNC embed environmental redress, community reinvestment, and youth leadership into climate justice legislation—transforming climate action into a vehicle for structural equity and intergenerational repair.

Background

  • Black and Brown youth are 2–5 times more likely to grow up near polluting industries.
  • Federal programs, such as the New Deal and interstate highway system, excluded many communities of color.
  • Tribal youth suffer from climate displacement and water insecurity linked to colonial land use.
  • Climate funding often bypasses frontline communities in implementation phases.
  • Youth climate coalitions have endorsed reparative frameworks, including land return and direct investment.

Analysis

  • Failing to address the past undermines trust in Democratic climate leadership.
  • Reparative climate policy offers a narrative of justice, not just decarbonization.
  • Embedding equity into implementation is key for local legitimacy and long-term outcomes.
  • Reframing climate spending as redress can help unify justice, youth, and progressive coalitions.

Recommendations

Environmental Justice Reparations Fund

  • Direct federal climate investments to historically marginalized neighborhoods, with youth participation in allocation.

Land and Water Back to Indigenous Youth

  • Support tribal youth councils in reclaiming and stewarding sacred sites threatened by climate change.

Youth-Led Health Impact Studies

  • Fund youth-centered research into climate-linked illnesses in frontline communities.

Climate Civilian Corps for Reparative Work

  • Establish a paid corps for youth to lead cleanup, tree planting, and resilience building in high-burden zip codes.

Conclusion

Climate justice without redress is incomplete. The DNC must boldly acknowledge environmental harm and invest in youth-led repair. This is not just about carbon—it’s about dignity, memory, and healing through climate action. Reparations can be the bridge that brings climate policy to the people who need it most.
