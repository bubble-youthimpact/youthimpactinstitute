Policy Memo 33

Subject
Abolishing Life Without Parole Sentences for Minors
Date
April 06, 2024

Executive Summary

Sentencing youth to life without parole denies the possibility of rehabilitation and violates international standards of justice. This memo urges UNESCO to launch a global initiative advocating the abolition of juvenile life sentences and promoting developmentally appropriate sentencing models.

Background

Scientific consensus shows that adolescent brains are still developing—particularly in areas related to impulse control and moral reasoning. Despite this, youth in over 40 countries can be sentenced to life imprisonment.
In the U.S. alone:

  • Over 1,300 people are serving life without parole (LWOP) for crimes committed as minors.
  • Black youth are disproportionately sentenced under LWOP statutes.

Such sentences contradict both neuroscience and the principle of human rights.

Analysis

Underlying problems:
Retributive Sentencing: Many legal systems still favor punishment over rehabilitation.

  • Political Fear: Lawmakers avoid reform due to “tough on crime” optics.
  • Judicial Discretion: Courts often lack guidance or bias training.
  • Lack of Review: Many systems fail to provide mechanisms for sentence reconsideration.

UNESCO must champion justice that reflects growth and redemption.

Recommendations

UNESCO should:

Global Advocacy Campaign

  • Mobilize member states to abolish juvenile LWOP sentences.
  • Align national sentencing laws with international children’s rights frameworks.

Judicial Education

  • Develop sentencing guidelines that reflect adolescent development.
  • Provide training for judges and prosecutors on neurodevelopmental science.

Resentencing and Release

  • Promote automatic parole reviews after 10–15 years for youth offenders.
  • Fund community reentry programs that support education and employment.

Storytelling and Healing

  • Support platforms for those sentenced to life as minors to share their stories.
  • Invest in victim–offender restorative justice processes where appropriate.

Conclusion

Children are not beyond change. By opposing life sentences for minors, UNESCO can protect the sanctity of youth and affirm that no future should be written off—no matter the past.
