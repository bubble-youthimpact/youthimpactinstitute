Policy Memo 130

Subject
Protecting Election Workers from Harassment and Political Violence
Date
January 22, 2025

Executive Summary

In recent election cycles, poll workers, election administrators, and volunteers—especially those in battleground states—have faced unprecedented levels of harassment, intimidation, and threats. This hostile environment threatens the integrity and staffing of future elections. This memo proposes a coordinated federal and state response to protect election personnel by criminalizing targeted threats, expanding physical and digital security, funding mental health support, launching a national recruitment campaign, and enhancing transparency around election protection. The survival of democratic institutions hinges on protecting those who operate them.

Background

  • Over 1 in 5 election officials have considered leaving their positions due to threats or stress.
  • Local election offices report difficulty recruiting poll workers and staff, especially younger and bilingual individuals.
  • Social media has amplified targeted harassment and doxing of election personnel.
  • Federal protections for election workers are minimal and inconsistently enforced.
  • Harassment disproportionately impacts women and officials of color, exacerbating representation issues.
  • A chilling effect on staff morale and recruitment endangers electoral functionality in 2026 and 2028.

Analysis

When election workers face threats without adequate protection, democratic processes are compromised. The erosion of trust and capacity within election administration can result in delays, errors, and public doubt in outcomes. Federal intervention is critical to standardize protections, while states must implement preventative measures. Rebuilding a culture of civic respect and volunteerism around elections—especially among youth—will help reverse current trends.

Recommendations

Criminalize Threats and Intimidation Targeting Election Workers

  • Pass federal legislation making threats against election officials a specific felony offense, with enhanced penalties for coordinated campaigns.
  • Direct the Department of Justice (DOJ) to prioritize investigations into harassment cases.

Expand Security Resources and Protocols

  • Allocate Homeland Security grants to upgrade physical and cyber protection for election offices.
  • Offer election officials secure communication lines, anonymous data protection, and threat response training.

Provide Mental Health Support for Election Personnel

  • Fund confidential counseling and burnout-prevention programs for workers exposed to harassment and trauma.
  • Offer paid leave options for personnel under sustained threat.

Launch a National Youth-Focused Election Worker Recruitment Campaign

  • Create civic engagement programs in high schools and colleges to prepare young people for nonpartisan poll work.
  • Partner with Gen Z-targeted media and creators to normalize election volunteering.

Enhance Public Transparency and Trust in Electoral Systems

  • Fund public awareness campaigns highlighting the professionalism and impartiality of election workers.
  • Combat disinformation with a national election facts hub updated in real-time during voting seasons.

Protect Marginalized Election Workers from Targeted Abuse

  • Offer legal support and escalation resources for election officials facing harassment based on race, gender, or immigration status.
  • Ensure harassment response protocols are trauma-informed and culturally competent.

Conclusion

Democracy cannot function without the people who run it. Protecting election workers from violence and intimidation is a moral and political imperative. By strengthening legal protections, supporting staff wellbeing, and rebuilding public trust, the DNC and its partners can help ensure secure and functional elections in 2026, 2028, and beyond—while sending a powerful message that political violence has no place in American democracy.
