Policy Memo 144

Subject
Abolishing Lifetime Judicial Appointments Through Term Limits for Federal Judges
Date
March 05, 2025

Executive Summary

Lifetime appointments to the federal judiciary foster judicial entrenchment, reduce accountability, and disconnect judges from the evolving values of the electorate. This memo proposes a constitutional amendment establishing term limits—such as 18 years—for Supreme Court and appellate judges. Term limits would preserve judicial independence while ensuring generational turnover and public trust. This reform enjoys broad bipartisan support among voters and would modernize a stagnant feature of U.S. governance.

Background

The U.S. is the only major democracy that grants lifetime tenure to its highest court judges. Originally designed to ensure judicial independence, lifetime appointments now enable partisan capture, encourage strategic retirements, and reduce ideological balance. Implementing term limits would promote regular judicial turnover and enhance legitimacy.

  • Supreme Court justices now serve an average of 28 years, up from 15 in 1960.
  • Over 80% of Americans support term limits for Supreme Court justices.
  • Life tenure incentivizes appointments based on youth and ideological loyalty rather than judicial excellence.
  • Constitutional scholars have proposed 18-year staggered terms, with appointments every two years.
  • Article III of the Constitution requires “good behavior” tenure, but legal scholars agree Congress can regulate terms through amendment or judicial act.

Analysis

  • Term limits reduce the stakes of each judicial nomination and lower partisan warfare.
  • Regularized turnover ensures courts better reflect societal shifts and generational perspectives.
  • Judicial independence can be preserved with non-renewable fixed terms.
  • Critics fear politicization, but term limits actually increase predictability and transparency.
  • Amendment process will require cross-party consensus and long-term advocacy.

Recommendations

Propose a Judicial Term Limits Amendment

  • Introduce a constitutional amendment to establish 18-year non-renewable terms for federal appellate and Supreme Court justices.
  • Include staggered appointment schedules to ensure equitable distribution across presidential terms.

Transitional Implementation Plan

  • Phase in term limits gradually to avoid immediate court destabilization.
  • Allow current justices to continue under existing rules, while applying new limits prospectively.

Strengthen Public Education on Reform

  • Launch bipartisan public engagement campaigns to explain judicial reform.
  • Fund civic education efforts to contextualize Article III and modern court dynamics.

Create a Commission for Judicial Accountability

  • Establish a bipartisan oversight commission to monitor implementation, address ethics concerns, and ensure public trust.

Conclusion

Judicial power should not be a lifetime entitlement. Term limits would preserve the judiciary’s integrity while ensuring it evolves with the democratic society it serves. By abolishing life tenure, Congress can promote fairness, reduce political gamesmanship, and reinvigorate public trust in the courts.
