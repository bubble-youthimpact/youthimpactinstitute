Policy Memo 113

Subject
Expanding Federal Grant Programs to Boost Youth-Led Startups in Underserved Communities
Date
December 02, 2024

Executive Summary

Youth entrepreneurship holds transformative potential to stimulate economic growth and social innovation, especially in underserved communities where access to capital is limited. However, federal grant programs targeting youth-led startups remain underfunded and insufficiently tailored to the unique challenges young entrepreneurs face. This memo advocates for expanding and redesigning federal grant initiatives to increase accessibility, equity, and impact, particularly for minority and rural youth. By channeling resources to youth entrepreneurs, the SBA can foster economic inclusion, job creation, and community resilience.

Background

  • Youth entrepreneurship is a key driver of innovation and job creation but faces systemic barriers including capital scarcity, lack of mentorship, and limited market access.
  • Underserved communities, including rural areas and communities of color, have disproportionately low participation in federal startup grants.
  • Existing federal grants often require complex application processes and do not account for youth-specific challenges, such as limited credit history or business experience.
  • Federal initiatives, such as the Youth Entrepreneurship Act (2019), laid groundwork but need scale and modernization to reach more youth effectively.

Analysis

Expanding federal grants with youth-focused criteria can address economic disparities by:

  • Providing accessible funding to young entrepreneurs who lack traditional collateral or business history.
  • Encouraging innovative business models that tackle community-specific challenges.
  • Stimulating local economies through job creation and new market development.
  • However, without simplification of grant processes and targeted outreach, many youth may remain excluded.
  • Partnerships with local organizations are critical to identifying promising youth entrepreneurs and delivering grant assistance effectively.

Recommendations

Federal grant programs should:

  • Increase dedicated funding pools specifically for youth-led startups in underserved regions.
  • Simplify application processes with youth-friendly language and support services.
  • Incorporate equity metrics to prioritize grants for minority, rural, and disabled youth entrepreneurs.
  • Partner with community-based organizations to co-design grant outreach and technical assistance.
  • Establish pilot programs with flexible funding models (e.g., microgrants, matched grants) to test innovative approaches.
  • Monitor and publicly report on youth grant outcomes to ensure transparency and continuous improvement.

Conclusion

Federal grant programs are pivotal levers to unlock the economic potential of youth entrepreneurship in underserved communities. By expanding and reforming these programs with an equity lens, the SBA can foster a new generation of innovators and job creators. This not only supports individual youth success but also contributes to broader economic resilience and social equity nationwide.
