Policy Memo 65

Subject
Addressing Employment Discrimination Against Neurodivergent Youth
Date
July 11, 2024

Executive Summary

Neurodivergent youth face significant barriers in accessing and retaining employment due to systemic bias, inaccessible hiring practices, and lack of workplace accommodations. This memo recommends that the ILO adopt enforceable neurodiversity employment standards, fund youth-led workplace innovation hubs, and mandate inclusive hiring frameworks across member states. Employment equity must extend to cognitive and neurological differences.

Background

Employment outcomes for neurodivergent youth remain alarmingly low:

  • Autistic young adults face unemployment rates exceeding 80% in some countries (WHO, 2023).
  • Hiring systems favor candidates with strong social fluency and conformity—traits that can disadvantage neurodivergent applicants.
  • Disclosure of neurodivergent status can lead to bias or exclusion.
  • Accommodations are often unavailable or denied despite legal mandates.

Analysis

Current employment systems are built on narrow definitions of productivity and communication:

  • Hiring Bias: Interviews and resumes prioritize interpersonal charm and “cultural fit” over job-relevant skills.
  • Lack of Mentorship: Neurodivergent youth rarely access networks and mentoring that are essential to workplace success.
  • Disclosure Dilemmas: Stigma and retaliation discourage transparency about access needs.
  • Rigid Work Structures: Standard 9–5 formats, open-plan offices, and sensory overload create hostile environments.

Recommendations

Inclusive Hiring and Legal Enforcement

  • Issue ILO directives on inclusive hiring benchmarks for neurodivergent candidates.
  • Establish a compliance mechanism for reasonable accommodations under disability employment laws.

Supportive Work Environments

  • Promote flexible scheduling, task-based metrics, and sensory accommodations.
  • Encourage neurodivergent-led design of inclusive workplace policies.

Youth Innovation and Visibility

  • Fund youth-designed employment platforms for neurodivergent workers.
  • Launch a global media campaign to destigmatize neurodivergent talent in the workforce.

Conclusion

True employment equity requires redesigning systems to include—not just tolerate—neurological diversity. By adopting neurodiversity-focused standards, the ILO can champion a future where cognitive differences are seen not as deficits, but as assets.
