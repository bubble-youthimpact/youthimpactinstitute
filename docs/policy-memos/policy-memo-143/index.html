Policy Memo 143

Subject
Establishing a National Youth Voting Rights Act to Prevent Suppression on College Campuses
Date
March 02, 2025

Executive Summary

Student voters, especially those attending out-of-state colleges or living on campus, face disproportionate barriers to accessing the ballot. Confusing residency requirements, restricted polling places, and state voter ID laws threaten to suppress youth turnout. This memo proposes a National Youth Voting Rights Act to standardize protections for college students, including campus polling mandates and ID recognition. Enacting this policy would safeguard democratic participation for millions of young voters and ensure states uphold the constitutional right to vote.

Background

Despite high civic interest, young voters face institutional barriers that dampen electoral participation. College campuses—especially those in battleground states—have seen systematic polling closures and legal confusion over student residency and ID validity. A federal standard could preemptively address these issues and reinforce equal access for transient or out-of-state students.

  • In the 2022 midterms, campus-based polling locations were reduced in key states, such as Texas, Florida, and Georgia.
  • At least 13 states restrict student ID use for voting.
  • The average wait time to vote on campuses is significantly longer than in non-student areas.
  • Legal uncertainty around student residency disproportionately affects students from marginalized backgrounds.
  • Federal law does not guarantee polling access at or near universities.

Analysis

  • Suppressing college voters diminishes representation for one of the most diverse and civically engaged generations.
  • Voter ID laws disproportionately affect racial minorities, low-income students, and out-of-state youth.
  • Fragmented state laws create confusion and deter turnout.
  • A federal law would set consistent expectations across jurisdictions.
  • Increased youth participation strengthens long-term civic health and trust in democratic institutions.

Recommendations

Campus Polling Access Mandate

  • Require all public universities with over 3,000 students to host an on-campus polling site.
  • Mandate extended early voting hours on campuses during federal elections.

Federal Recognition of Student IDs

  • Require states to accept accredited student ID cards for voter identification in federal elections.
  • Fund secure barcode standards for student ID issuance via the Department of Education.

Residency Clarity and Protections

  • Affirm students’ right to register to vote where they attend school without additional documentation.
  • Create a national hotline to assist youth voters with campus-related voting concerns.

Monitoring and Enforcement

  • Authorize the Department of Justice to investigate and prosecute campus voter suppression.
  • Direct the Election Assistance Commission (EAC) to collect disaggregated data on campus access.

Conclusion

Access to the ballot box should not depend on whether a young American attends college in their home state or across the country. By passing the National Youth Voting Rights Act, Congress can eliminate campus-based voter suppression and uphold equal democratic participation. Protecting student voters isn’t just about convenience—it’s about ensuring that youth voices shape the future they will inherit.
