Policy Memo 23

Subject
Regulating Deepfakes to Protect Youth from Manipulation and Harassment
Date
March 07, 2024

Executive Summary

Deepfake technologies pose a growing threat to youth safety, democracy, and truth itself. From misinformation to non-consensual explicit content, young people are both targets and unwitting spreaders of AI-generated fakes. This memo recommends that UNESCO develop educational frameworks, legal models, and global norms to mitigate deepfake harms while promoting youth awareness and digital literacy.

Background

Deepfakes—AI-generated synthetic videos or images—are becoming more realistic, accessible, and weaponized.
Youth face particular risks:

  • Non-consensual deepfake pornography disproportionately targets teenage girls.
  • Political deepfakes mislead first-time voters and student activists.
  • Bullying and extortion via fake media are on the rise in schools and online communities.

Legal responses are fragmented, and many platforms do not proactively detect or remove harmful content. Without coordinated regulation and education, deepfakes threaten youth rights, safety, and trust in information ecosystems.

Analysis

Major concerns include:
Psychological Harm: Victims of explicit or bullying deepfakes report trauma and social isolation.

  • Democratic Risk: Political deepfakes erode civic trust, especially among first-time voters.
  • Educational Lag: Most schools do not teach youth how to identify synthetic media.
  • Regulatory Gaps: Most countries lack specific laws addressing deepfake creation or distribution.

This rapidly evolving threat demands proactive, youth-centered regulatory action.

Recommendations

UNESCO should:

Global Norms and Laws

  • Propose a model legal framework for member states criminalizing malicious deepfakes.
  • Advocate for a youth bill of rights in the era of synthetic media.

Platform Accountability

  • Promote detection standards and mandatory takedown policies for tech companies.
  • Encourage watermarking and labeling of AI-generated content.

Education and Resilience

  • Develop curricula to teach youth deepfake detection and verification skills.
  • Fund peer-led workshops and media literacy labs in schools.

Victim Support

  • Support global hotlines and legal aid for youth targeted by deepfake abuse.
  • Develop digital trauma resources and school-based counseling programs.

Conclusion

Deepfakes are not just a technological issue—they are a youth protection issue. UNESCO can lead the world in safeguarding truth, privacy, and dignity by regulating synthetic media and preparing the next generation to navigate a world of digital deception.
