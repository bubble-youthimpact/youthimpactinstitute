Policy Memo 122

Subject
Expanding Nutrition Assistance Programs Tailored for Disabled College Students
Date
December 29, 2024

Executive Summary

Disabled college students face compounded barriers to food security, including limited income, accessibility challenges, and higher living costs. This memo recommends expanding nutrition assistance programs with tailored provisions addressing the unique needs of disabled students. Enhancing accessibility and benefit design will reduce disparities and support equitable educational attainment.

Background

  • Disabled students have higher rates of food insecurity than their non-disabled peers.
  • Current nutrition programs rarely account for accessibility barriers or additional costs faced by disabled students.
  • Many disabled students rely on limited income and face challenges accessing meal programs on campus.
  • Inclusive policy design remains limited in federal nutrition assistance initiatives.

Analysis

Tailored nutrition assistance can:

  • Address accessibility barriers, such as transportation and dietary restrictions.
  • Provide additional benefits reflecting higher living expenses and care needs.
  • Reduce dropout rates and improve health outcomes among disabled students.
  • However, program modifications require interagency coordination and funding increases.

Recommendations

HHS should:

  • Amend nutrition assistance eligibility criteria to explicitly include disabled college students.
  • Develop accessible application and benefit delivery systems (e.g., home delivery options).
  • Coordinate with the Department of Education to integrate support services.
  • Fund pilot projects testing innovative models of assistance for disabled students.

Conclusion

Expanding nutrition assistance with a focus on disabled college students is crucial for equitable food security. HHS leadership in adapting programs will ensure no student is left behind due to disability-related barriers.
