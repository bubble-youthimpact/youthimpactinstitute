Policy Memo 79

Subject
Ensuring Digital Inclusion for Older Adults
Date
August 22, 2024

Executive Summary

As digital services expand across public life, older adults face growing exclusion due to limited access and digital literacy. This memo urges the ITU to implement a global older adult digital inclusion initiative, with infrastructure, education, and age-friendly design as cornerstones.

Background

  • Over 60% of people aged 65+ in low- and middle-income countries lack internet access.
  • Essential services—from banking to healthcare—are increasingly online-only.
  • Cognitive and physical accessibility issues are rarely considered in app or interface design.
  • Ageism in digital spaces perpetuates exclusion and self-censorship.

Analysis

Older adults' digital exclusion is multidimensional:

  • Infrastructure Gap: Older adults in rural and low-income areas often lack broadband access.
  • Design Inaccessibility: Interfaces assume digital fluency and overlook vision, motor, and memory changes.
  • Digital Literacy Barriers: Training programs rarely target older users, particularly in non-urban areas.
  • Data Privacy Risks: Seniors are often targeted by fraud or phishing, with limited recourse.

Recommendations

Infrastructure Expansion

  • Fund age-focused internet access programs with senior centers as digital hubs.
  • Partner with telecom companies for discounted elder connectivity plans.

User-Centered Design Standards

  • Develop age-friendly digital interface guidelines and universal design protocols.
  • Certify digital tools that meet accessibility standards for older users.

Targeted Education

  • Create public digital literacy curricula tailored to seniors.
  • Support intergenerational tech training programs.

Conclusion

Digital exclusion of older adults is an urgent equity issue. ITU can lead global efforts to ensure that longevity in the digital age means participation, not marginalization.
