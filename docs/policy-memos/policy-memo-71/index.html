UN Policy Memos

Policy Memo 71

Subject
Ending the Deportation of Unaccompanied Minors: A Rights-Based Approach
Date
July 29, 2024

Executive Summary

Despite international conventions guaranteeing protections for children, many states continue to detain and deport unaccompanied minors in violation of their best interests. This memo urges the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child to initiate a global review of deportation practices targeting unaccompanied minors and to enforce new legal standards prohibiting child expulsion, especially to conflict zones or unstable environments.

Background

Deportation of unaccompanied minors persists despite human rights treaties:

  • Over 130,000 unaccompanied minors sought asylum in 2023, many in regions lacking legal protections.
  • The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) mandates that state actions prioritize the "best interests of the child."
  • In practice, many countries deport minors without due process, child-sensitive interviews, or legal representation.
  • Unaccompanied youth face arbitrary detention, expedited removals, and pushbacks at sea or land borders.

Analysis

Youth deportation undermines child rights systems:

  • Legal Grey Zones: Children often fall through cracks between immigration and child welfare systems.
  • Risk of Re-trafficking or Violence: Many minors are deported to countries where they face persecution, recruitment, or exploitation.
  • Psychological Harm: Deportation re-traumatizes youth already affected by displacement, war, or family separation.
  • Lack of Voice: Youth are excluded from legal proceedings that determine their fate, violating participatory rights.

Recommendations

Absolute Prohibition of Child Deportation

  • Encourage the CRC to issue General Comment prohibiting deportation of unaccompanied minors to unsafe conditions.
  • Recommend that states create a legal firewall between immigration enforcement and child protection services.

Child-Centered Legal Protections

  • Require age-appropriate, trauma-informed interviews and free legal counsel for all migrant children.
  • Mandate child protection assessments before any return decision is made.

International Accountability and Oversight

  • Establish a CRC review mechanism focused on state compliance with youth deportation protections.
  • Support community-based shelter and guardianship alternatives to detention or deportation.

Conclusion

The deportation of unaccompanied youth is a systemic failure of global child protection. The CRC must act to reinforce the principle that no child should be forcibly returned to danger. A world that respects the rights of young migrants must begin by keeping them safe, heard, and protected.
