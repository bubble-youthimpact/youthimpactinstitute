Policy Memo 68

Subject
Ensuring Neurodivergent Inclusion in Refugee Protection Systems
Date
July 20, 2024

Executive Summary

Refugee protection frameworks often overlook the needs of neurodivergent individuals, particularly children and youth. This invisibility leads to inaccessible services, misdiagnoses, and neglect in shelters, education, and asylum processes. This memo recommends that UNHCR formally integrate neurodiversity into its protection policies, establish accommodation standards, and partner with refugee-led disability organizations to design inclusive practices.

Background

Neurodivergent refugees face multilayered exclusion:

  • There is no standardized screening or support for neurodevelopmental conditions in most refugee camps.
  • Behaviors stemming from neurodivergence are often misinterpreted as trauma responses, disobedience, or mental illness.
  • Language and cultural differences further obscure accurate understanding of neurodivergence.
  • Education, food distribution, and shelter systems are rarely designed with sensory or processing accommodations.

Analysis

Refugee systems reproduce ableist exclusion:

  • Invisible Needs: Without diagnostic access or advocacy, neurodivergent youth remain unsupported and misunderstood.
  • Barrier-Rich Services: Long queues, noisy environments, and rigid protocols exacerbate stress and make services unusable.
  • Cultural Erasure: Refugee policy often reflects Western-centric models of disability, ignoring local knowledge and dignity frameworks.
  • Stateless and Voiceless: Undocumented or displaced neurodivergent youth often have no legal identity or protective status.

Recommendations

Inclusive Protection Standards

  • UNHCR should develop neurodiversity-inclusive guidelines for camp services, education, and case management.
  • Include mandatory sensory accommodations and flexible communication protocols in humanitarian programming.

Capacity Building and Representation

  • Partner with disability justice organizations, especially those led by displaced people, to co-design inclusive responses.
  • Train refugee camp staff to recognize and support neurodivergence in culturally sensitive ways.

Legal Recognition and Advocacy

  • Advocate for asylum frameworks that recognize disability-based persecution, including neurodivergent marginalization.
  • Support neurodivergent youth to participate in refugee consultation processes and leadership councils.

Conclusion

Neurodivergent refugees deserve protection, dignity, and voice. UNHCR has the responsibility and reach to ensure its systems do not replicate the same marginalization that many fled. A neurodiversity-informed refugee policy is not just humane—it is essential to upholding the universality of human rights.
