Policy Memo 30

Subject
Supporting Indigenous Youth in Incarceration Through Culturally Grounded Justice
Date
March 28, 2024

Executive Summary

Indigenous youth are disproportionately incarcerated in many countries and face systems that erase their identity and disrupt their communities. This memo recommends that UNESCO promote culturally grounded, community-based justice approaches that support healing and self-determination for Indigenous youth.

Background

In countries, such as Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, Indigenous youth represent a shocking proportion of incarcerated minors—often 5 to 15 times their population share.
Root causes include:

  • Intergenerational trauma from colonization and residential schools.
  • Discriminatory policing and lack of culturally appropriate legal representation.
  • Displacement from land, language, and ceremony.

Prison further isolates Indigenous youth from their communities and traditions.

Analysis

Key barriers include:

  • Cultural Erasure: Detention often prohibits spiritual practices and cultural contact.
  • Systemic Racism: Courts and prisons apply harsher penalties to Indigenous youth.
  • Colonial Justice Models: Incarceration ignores Indigenous concepts of justice and restoration.
  • Lack of Data Sovereignty: Communities have little control over justice-related data.

UNESCO must act to protect Indigenous cultural survival within justice systems.

Recommendations

UNESCO should:

Cultural Safety Standards

  • Require youth facilities to accommodate ceremony, language, and Indigenous spiritual leaders.
  • Train staff in Indigenous histories, rights, and trauma-informed care.

Alternative Justice Pathways

  • Promote community-led justice circles, land-based programs, and elder-led diversion efforts.
  • Support funding for Indigenous-led youth rehabilitation centers.

Policy Collaboration

  • Work with Indigenous nations to co-create international justice standards.
  • Respect Indigenous data governance and knowledge systems.

Visibility and Voice

  • Amplify Indigenous youth advocates at global justice and education forums.
  • Support storytelling, art, and language projects led by incarcerated Indigenous youth.

Conclusion

True justice cannot exist without cultural survival. UNESCO must uphold Indigenous rights in every detention context and help build systems rooted in healing, not punishment.
