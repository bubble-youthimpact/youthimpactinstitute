Policy Memo 150

Subject
Capping Corporate PAC Donations in Youth-Impacting Industries
Date
June 04, 2025

Executive Summary

Corporate Political Action Committees (PACs) tied to fossil fuels, private prisons, for-profit education, and student lenders disproportionately shape policy affecting youth futures. This memo recommends setting strict federal caps on PAC donations from such industries to reduce undue influence and restore democratic accountability.

Background

While direct corporate donations are banned, affiliated PACs legally contribute millions to candidates.

  • Industries, such as fossil fuels, private prisons, and student lending, lobby aggressively to maintain status quo policies that harm youth.
  • Corporate PACs from these sectors have poured over $1 billion into federal races in the last decade.
  • Youth have little voice in campaign finance decisions but bear the consequences in environmental, educational, and economic policy.
  • Growing youth-led movements demand campaign finance reform to restore policy alignment with public—not corporate—interest.

Analysis

  • Targeted donation caps for harmful sectors preserve free speech while limiting outsized influence.
  • Reducing fossil fuel and prison PAC donations aligns with popular youth demands.
  • Critics may claim overreach, but precedent exists for industry-specific finance limits (e.g., foreign nationals, federal contractors).
  • Transparency alone is insufficient—structural limits are necessary.

Recommendations

Federal Legislation

  • Pass the Youth Futures Protection in Campaign Finance Act to cap PAC donations from designated industries at $5,000 per cycle per candidate.

Industry Designation Criteria

  • Use youth impact assessments to determine which industries are subject to the cap (e.g., environmental, educational, incarceration harm).

Strengthen FEC Enforcement

  • Boost FEC capacity to audit, monitor, and penalize violators.

Public Campaign Matching Fund

  • Expand small-dollar matching funds to reduce candidate reliance on corporate PACs.

Conclusion

Capping PAC donations in industries that shape youth futures is not just campaign finance reform—it’s generational justice. By limiting the influence of profit-driven sectors, Congress can reorient policymaking toward the public good and youth wellbeing.
