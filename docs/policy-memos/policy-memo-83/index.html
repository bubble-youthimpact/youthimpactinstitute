Policy Memo 83

Subject
Climate Adaptation for Older Populations in Urban Heat Zones
Date
September 03, 2024

Executive Summary

Extreme heat disproportionately threatens older adults, who account for the vast majority of heat-related deaths. This memo calls on UNEP to integrate age-specific protections into urban climate adaptation plans, with special attention to cooling access, mobility, and disaster planning.

Background

  • By 2050, 70% of the world’s older population will live in cities, many in low-income or informal housing.
  • Older adults are physiologically more vulnerable to heat stress and air pollution.
  • Many cities lack heat action plans that address older people’s needs (e.g., mobility issues, isolation).
  • Cooling infrastructure (e.g., shade, water access) is often concentrated in wealthier districts.

Analysis

Urban climate policies currently fail older adults by:

  • Overlooking Access: Heat warnings are often digital, inaccessible to those without phones or literacy.
  • Neglecting Isolation: Older people living alone may not receive community check-ins or transportation help.
  • Designing Inaccessibly: Cooling shelters may lack elevators, ramps, or adequate toilets.
  • Underfunding Long-Term Change: Short-term responses ignore the need for structural urban redesign for aging bodies.

Recommendations

Cooling and Resilience Infrastructure

  • Require shaded walkways, water stations, and green roofs in city planning.
  • Prioritize cooling access in elderly care facilities and public housing.

Inclusive Communication and Mobility

  • Develop analog-friendly alert systems (e.g., radio, door-to-door health workers).
  • Provide accessible transit to cooling centers.

Climate-Aging Urban Metrics

  • Create “heat vulnerability maps” that include age as a key metric.
  • Fund city-based research centers on climate and aging.

Conclusion

UNEP must ensure that climate adaptation is not youth-centered by default. Aging urban populations require intentional design and justice-driven responses to survive the heatwaves of tomorrow.
