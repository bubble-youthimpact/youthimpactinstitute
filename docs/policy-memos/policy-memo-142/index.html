Policy Memo 142

Subject
Establishing a Federal Youth Neurodiversity Council to Guide Inclusive Policy
Date
February 27, 2025

Executive Summary

Youth with autism, ADHD, learning differences, and other neurodivergent experiences remain underrepresented in federal policymaking—even when decisions directly affect their education, employment, and healthcare. This memo proposes the formation of a Federal Youth Neurodiversity Council to advise executive agencies and Congress on inclusive and equitable policy design.

Background

Neurodivergent youth disproportionately face school exclusion, unemployment, and criminalization. Current disability advisory bodies rarely include youth perspectives or lived neurodivergent experience. Youth neurodiversity advocates have led local reforms on inclusive classrooms, police training, and accessible tech. Models, such as the President’s Advisory Commission on Asian Americans or the Youth Climate Corps Advisory Council, demonstrate that youth advisory councils can shape federal priorities.
Challenges:

  • Existing neurodiversity frameworks often exclude multiply marginalized youth.
  • Many neurodivergent youth distrust systems due to past trauma in school or healthcare settings.

Analysis

  • Inclusive policymaking must include neurodivergent youth in leadership, not just consultation.
  • This Council would provide input across education, labor, criminal justice, and healthcare.
  • Youth-led policy generates more innovative, responsive, and human-centered outcomes.
  • Federal recognition would validate neurodivergent identity as a strength, not pathology.

Recommendations

Create the Federal Youth Neurodiversity Council

  • Include 15–20 youth (ages 15–24) representing diverse neurodivergent identities and geographies.
  • House the Council within the Office of Public Engagement with rotating co-chairs.

Ensure Accessibility and Compensation

  • Pay all Council members a federal stipend.
  • Provide communication access, sensory-friendly meeting formats, and flexible participation options.

Institutionalize Input into Policy Processes

  • Require agencies (DOE, DOJ, HHS) to consult the Council on new regulations.
  • Include Council representation on federal disability and education task forces.

Conclusion

Nothing about us without us. A Federal Youth Neurodiversity Council would transform disability policy by grounding it in lived expertise. It would also send a powerful message: neurodivergent youth belong at the table of American democracy.
