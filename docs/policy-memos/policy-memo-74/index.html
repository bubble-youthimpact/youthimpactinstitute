Policy Memo 74

Subject
Expanding the Refugee Definition to Include Climate-Affected Youth
Date
August 07, 2024

Executive Summary

UNHCR’s mandate must evolve to meet the challenges of 21st-century displacement. This memo proposes an expansion of the refugee definition to include youth forced to flee due to climate change, particularly when state failure, infrastructure collapse, or violence follows environmental degradation. Protecting these youth requires legal innovation and urgent political will.

Background

The limitations of current refugee law exclude many climate-affected youth:

  • The 1951 Refugee Convention defines refugees as fleeing “persecution,” not environmental collapse.
  • Youth displaced after slow-onset disasters (e.g. drought, desertification, sea-level rise) receive no international protections.
  • Stateless youth from disappearing island nations face legal extinction.
  • Overburdened asylum systems often reject climate-related claims outright.

Analysis

Climate displacement is inseparable from human rights and political instability:

  • Persecution via Neglect: States failing to act on climate threats can produce conditions akin to persecution.
  • Compound Vulnerabilities: Youth face intersecting threats—economic, ecological, and political.
  • Humanitarian Double Standards: Climate-affected youth are treated differently based on region and race.
  • Legal Precedent Exists: Cases, such as Teitiota v. New Zealand, have opened the door to climate-based refugee claims.

Recommendations

Legal Expansion and Clarification

  • Propose a new UNHCR guideline expanding “refugee” to include youth fleeing life-threatening climate conditions.
  • Urge regional blocs (e.g., the African Union) to update refugee protocols accordingly.

Protection Infrastructure

  • Create emergency humanitarian visa pathways for climate-displaced youth.
  • Establish regional youth relocation programs with legal status, housing, and education guarantees.

Climate Justice Integration

  • Align UNHCR’s climate strategy with youth climate justice movements.
  • Fund legal clinics specializing in climate-related asylum claims.

Conclusion

The line between persecution and climate collapse is blurring—especially for youth in fragile states. The UNHCR has a unique opportunity to lead legal reform and safeguard the rights of a generation facing forced migration in an age of environmental crisis.
