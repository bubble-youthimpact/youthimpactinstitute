Policy Memo 137

Subject
Establishing Universal Basic Internships to Address Unpaid Labor Inequities
Date
February 12, 2025

Executive Summary

Unpaid internships perpetuate privilege and obstruct economic mobility. Universal Basic Internships (UBIs)—publicly funded stipends for students engaged in internships—would democratize access to professional experience and diversify leadership pipelines. This memo proposes a federal UBI framework and urges the DNC to include it in its 2026 youth economic empowerment platform.

Background

  • Over 40% of internships in the U.S. are unpaid, disproportionately benefiting wealthier students.
  • BIPOC, first-generation, and rural youth face barriers to unpaid positions in politics, media, and STEM.
  • Paid internships correlate strongly with future employment and higher salaries.
  • States, such as New Jersey, have piloted public internship stipend programs.

Analysis

Internships are often prerequisites for competitive employment, yet only those who can afford unpaid labor reap the benefits. This structural barrier exacerbates inequality in public service, tech, academia, and beyond. Universal Basic Internships would provide dignity and access to all students—particularly those in community colleges, HBCUs, and trade programs—while building a more representative workforce.

Recommendations

Federal UBI Program Design

  • Introduce the Public Internship Access Act to fund $4,000–$6,000 stipends per qualifying student.
  • Cover internships at nonprofits, public agencies, startups, and mission-driven businesses.

Youth Outreach and Equity Goals

  • Allocate 60% of UBI funds to low-income, first-gen, and minority youth.
  • Create dedicated UBI grants for students in rural areas or nontraditional programs.

Public-Private Funding Partnerships

  • Incentivize employers to match public funds or cover transportation/housing costs.
  • Provide tax credits for employers hosting paid interns from underrepresented backgrounds.

Support Infrastructure

  • Establish regional Internship Resource Hubs to assist with placements, mentorship, and legal protections.
  • Fund academic credit conversion and job guarantee pathways for internship completers.

Conclusion

Universal Basic Internships are not handouts—they are investments in economic mobility and future leadership. By championing this reform, the DNC can dismantle elitist barriers to opportunity and affirm that all youth deserve a fair start, regardless of background or bank account.
