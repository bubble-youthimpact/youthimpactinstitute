UN Policy Memos

Policy Memo 61

Subject
Advancing Comprehensive Sexuality Education for LGBTQ+ Youth in Faith-Based Schools
Date
June 29, 2024

Executive Summary

LGBTQ+ youth in faith-based educational institutions often lack access to inclusive sexuality education, contributing to stigma, misinformation, and mental health crises. While many states have committed to comprehensive sexuality education (CSE), religious schools are frequently exempted, creating a dangerous gap in protection. This memo urges UNFPA to lead a multi-stakeholder effort to ensure that faith-aligned curricula still uphold human rights standards. Strategies include model policy guidelines, interfaith youth dialogues, and rights-based training for educators.

Background

Comprehensive Sexuality Education (CSE) is a proven tool for improving health, empowerment, and safety—but LGBTQ+ youth in religious schools are often excluded due to institutional doctrine or legal loopholes.

  • LGBTQ+ students in religious schools are more likely to report depression, bullying, and suicidal ideation.
  • Over 60 countries allow religious institutions to opt out of national CSE mandates (ILGA, 2023).
  • Faith-based schools educate over 300 million youth globally—many in low-income regions where alternative sexual health resources are scarce.
  • A 2024 UNICEF study found that LGBTQ+ youth in faith-based schools had 40% lower knowledge of HIV prevention.

Analysis

Current CSE initiatives rarely engage faith communities as allies, framing religious education as oppositional. This has led to:

  • Polarization over Protection: National education ministries face pushback from religious groups when implementing LGBTQ+ inclusive content, often resulting in political compromises that dilute CSE.
  • Curricular Segregation: LGBTQ+ rights are excluded from curricula or treated as taboo, denying young people basic information about identity, consent, and health.
  • Lack of Faith-Literate Trainers: Teacher training rarely addresses how to reconcile human rights with religious values, leaving educators unprepared or resistant.
  • Missed Opportunities for Dialogue: Progressive voices within faith communities—especially among youth—are overlooked in CSE policymaking.

Recommendations

Model Inclusive Guidelines

  • Develop CSE curriculum templates that address sexuality within religious and cultural contexts while upholding rights.
  • Include optional modules on reconciling faith and identity, co-designed with youth and progressive theologians.

Faith-Based Youth Engagement

  • Create interfaith youth advisory groups to inform policy and localize messaging.
  • Fund storytelling platforms for LGBTQ+ students to safely share experiences and inform community-led change.

Teacher Training & Institutional Incentives

  • Offer professional development on trauma-informed and rights-based sexuality education for educators in faith-based institutions.
  • Provide funding incentives for schools that pilot inclusive CSE modules.

Conclusion

LGBTQ+ youth in religious schools are doubly marginalized—by societal stigma and educational neglect. UNFPA can bridge this gap by convening faith leaders, youth advocates, and education ministries to co-create models that are both culturally grounded and rights-respecting. Inclusive sexuality education in faith contexts is not only possible—it is essential for dignity, health, and equity.
