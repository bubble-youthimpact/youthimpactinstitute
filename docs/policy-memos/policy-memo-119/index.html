Policy Memo 119

Subject
Expanding SNAP Eligibility and Access for College Students to Reduce Food Insecurity
Date
December 20, 2024

Executive Summary

Food insecurity affects nearly half of college students nationwide, threatening academic success and well-being. Current SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) eligibility rules restrict many college students from receiving benefits. This memo recommends federal policy reforms to broaden eligibility criteria, streamline application processes, and increase outreach efforts. Expanding SNAP access for college students will address food insecurity at scale, improving health outcomes and academic persistence.

Background

  • Approximately 43% of college students report food insecurity.
  • SNAP currently excludes many full-time students unless they meet strict work or care responsibilities.
  • The administrative burden and lack of awareness prevent eligible students from applying.
  • Food insecurity correlates with lower GPA, higher dropout rates, and poor mental health.

Analysis

Broadening SNAP eligibility will:

  • Provide immediate food access for vulnerable students.
  • Improve academic outcomes by reducing hunger-related distractions and stress.
  • Help close equity gaps affecting low-income, minority, and first-generation college students.
  • However, existing policy inertia and stigma around public assistance pose barriers to reform and uptake.

Recommendations

USDA should:

  • Remove or relax work/study exemptions restricting student eligibility for SNAP.
  • Simplify application and renewal processes for students using digital tools.
  • Launch targeted outreach campaigns on campuses, especially at community colleges and minority-serving institutions.
  • Partner with colleges to provide enrollment support and nutritional education.
  • Monitor and report disaggregated data on student SNAP participation and outcomes.

Conclusion

Expanding SNAP eligibility and access for college students is a critical step toward combating food insecurity. USDA leadership in reforming these policies will ensure students have the nutrition necessary to succeed academically and personally.
