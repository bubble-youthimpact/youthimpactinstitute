Policy Memo 29

Subject
Digital Access as a Human Right for Incarcerated Youth
Date
March 25, 2024

Executive Summary

In today’s world, digital access is vital for education, communication, and reintegration. Yet youth in prison are systematically excluded from the digital sphere. This memo calls on UNESCO to define digital connectivity as a human right for incarcerated minors and to lead a global push for safe, educational internet access in juvenile justice systems.

Background

Globally, over 1 million youth are incarcerated, most without digital access.
Key issues include:

  • Barriers to remote learning, especially during crises.
  • Lack of access to legal resources, vocational training, or contact with family.
  • Reintegration difficulties due to digital illiteracy.

This exclusion deepens inequality and undermines rehabilitation.

Analysis

Core problems include:
Security Concerns: Governments fear misuse, but over-restrict access.

  • Outdated Infrastructure: Many detention centers lack basic digital tools.
  • Policy Gaps: Few global standards exist for digital rights of prisoners.
  • Reentry Risk: Youth are released into tech-dependent societies they can't navigate.

A new framework is needed that balances safety with digital empowerment.

Recommendations

UNESCO should:

Access and Tools

  • Promote the provision of filtered educational internet in youth facilities.
  • Support the distribution of secure digital devices for learning and legal access.

Digital Literacy

  • Fund tech education programs for incarcerated youth.
  • Include coding, critical media literacy, and internet safety.

Policy and Guidelines

  • Draft international standards for digital rights in incarceration.
  • Encourage governments to adopt rehabilitative digital policies.

Reentry Programs

  • Build reentry tech boot camps for digital reintegration.
  • Connect youth with mentors and job platforms online pre-release.

Conclusion

In the digital age, disconnection is a sentence beyond incarceration. By championing digital rights for youth in prison, UNESCO can ensure technology becomes a tool of liberation, not exclusion.
