Exploring Linguistics: An Impractical Field or a Fascinating Subject?

SeungWoo Sean Lee·June 17, 2024·1 min read

Linguistics is, broadly put, the scientific study of languages. At first glance, one might wrongly presume that linguistics is only a subject for polyglots, but, in reality, many great linguists are only monolinguals or bilinguals who harbor a love for languages.

Noam Chomsky is perhaps one of the most influential and popular linguists. Indeed, Chomsky revolutionized the whole field of linguistics by proposing the concept of Universal Grammar: he suggested that all human beings have a language acquisition device that enables them to learn languages easily.

Furthermore, linguistics is growing to be a popular field due to the advent of artificial intelligence. Studying linguistics can open doors to numerous career opportunities, as there is an increasing demand for NLP (natural language processing) engineers. If you want to land a job in, say, an AI firm, you might want to consider majoring in linguistics.

Globalization is a phenomenon that interests a lot of linguistics, for it has significantly impacted the spread of languages. English, for example, is now a dominant language in virtually every corner of the world (thanks to the economic and political power of the United States), which is putting many minority languages at the risk of becoming extinct.

Many linguistics and anthropologists work together to bring life to endangered languages. In most cases, linguists try to learn the language of indigenous communities and produce grammar books or dictionaries that could be passed down for generations.

We, as a society, are faced with a pressing issue. Language is not merely a means of communication, but a rich repository of beliefs, values, and traditions that should not be forgotten. We should thus work towards the goal of preventing as many languages from going extinct. Language preservation will - and should - forever be an important task for humanity.
