<p>After more than 50 years leading Berkshire Hathaway, Warren Buffett—one of the most famous and successful investors in the world—is officially retiring. At 94 years old, Buffett is stepping down, and while he’s leaving his job, he’s also leaving behind a legacy full of advice that people will remember for a long time.</p>
<h2>How Buffett Thought About Investing</h2>
<p>Warren Buffett didn’t try to get rich fast. Instead, he believed in something called value investing. That means he looked for strong companies that were being sold at a fair price and then held onto them for a long time—sometimes even forever.</p>
<p>He often said you should only invest in companies you understand and trust. That’s why he avoided most tech companies for years, only investing in Apple when he felt sure about it. He also warned people not to panic when the stock market goes up and down. He believed that being patient and calm was key to making smart decisions with money.</p>
<p>One of his biggest pieces of advice was to keep things simple. If you don’t want to pick individual stocks, he said it’s smart to just invest in a low-cost index fund that tracks the overall market. He believed that learning, reading, and understanding what you’re doing is more important than trying to “beat the market.”</p>
<h2>Who’s Taking Over?</h2>
<p>With Buffett retiring, his longtime teammate Greg Abel is taking over as CEO of Berkshire Hathaway. Abel has been with the company for years, helping run their energy business and managing big decisions behind the scenes.</p>
<p>Buffett has said many times that Abel is the right person to lead. He’s known for being hard-working, thoughtful, and focused on long-term success—just like Buffett. Many people feel confident that Abel will keep the company strong and stick to the same core values.</p>
<h2>More Than Just Money</h2>
<p>Warren Buffett wasn’t just known for being rich. He was also known for living a modest life. Even though he was a billionaire, he lived in the same house for decades, drove regular cars, and didn’t show off his money. He also promised to donate most of his fortune to charity and encouraged other billionaires to do the same.</p>
<p>In his letter announcing his retirement, Buffett reminded people to stay curious, work hard, and always keep learning. “The world has changed a lot since I started,” he wrote, “but the basic ideas of smart investing haven’t.”</p>
<p>So while he may be retiring, Warren Buffett’s ideas—and the example he set—will stick around for a long time.</p>
