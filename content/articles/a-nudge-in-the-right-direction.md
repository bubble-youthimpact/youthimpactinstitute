<p>The human mind is truly one of the greatest wonders in our world; it is truly a fine piece of machinery - so intricately constructed that it can accomplish tasks that even the all-mighty “Chat-GPT” can’t dare to do.</p>
<p>This begs the question: why do we all do dumb things on a regular basis? If we are all really smart, why do so many people on the internet use faulty logic? Why are we so easily swayed by misinformation?</p>
<p>Now, before we delve into this enigma, spend a few minutes to solve these 3 simple questions (should take you less than 1 minute).</p>

<p>If you made a mistake on any of these questions, you likely fell victim to your “system-1” thinking. System-1 thinking refers to a thought process that makes quick, autonomous judgments; this cognitive process contrasts from System-2 thinking, which makes more calculated decisions.</p>
<p>System 1 thinking is the root of almost all of our irrational decisions. Since it is so quick and automatic, it is also prone to cognitive biases that plague our rational thinking process.</p>
<p>Nudging tries to combat this irrational nature of humans by making small alterations in the decision-making environment and making sure that people aren’t led astray; an example of nudging can be simply changing the placement of foods in a cafeteria to encourage people to eat healthier (people would be most likely to eat foods that are placed at their eye level).</p>
<h2>Why do we need Nudge?</h2>
<p>The concept of psychological manipulation might conjure up images of wicked gaslighters or interrogators. However, it is safe to say that Nudge is not as evil as it might sound like. The whole purpose of Nudge is to ultimately steer people in the right direction. Since we are aware that people might make judgments that do not necessarily align with their long-term goals, we can slightly push, or “nudge,” them in a way that might be better for their wellbeing (and the wellbeing of their community).</p>
<p>It is also important to note that Nudge is different from coercion, which “forces” people to make certain decisions. Nudging only slightly changes the context in which people make decisions, leaving individuals free to make their own choices.</p>
