<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Youth turnout remains chronically low in federal elections, often due to structural barriers, such as class schedules, part-time jobs, or caregiving responsibilities. Declaring Election Day a federal holiday would remove key participation obstacles, foster a culture of civic duty, and enfranchise millions of young voters. This memo urges Congress to pass legislation establishing a national Voting Holiday for all federal elections.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Unlike many democracies, the U.S. holds federal elections on a Tuesday with no mandatory time off.</p>
<ul><li>College students and young workers often lack access to paid leave or flexible schedules.</li><li>Youth aged 18–29 vote at lower rates than older cohorts, citing time constraints as a major reason.</li><li>States that offer Election Day off as a holiday (e.g., Illinois, Kentucky) show higher youth turnout.</li><li>Making Election Day a national holiday sends a signal that voting is a shared civic responsibility.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Removing logistical barriers can significantly raise participation among youth and working-class voters.</li><li>A national holiday would reduce lines, administrative burden, and poll worker shortages.</li><li>Critics may argue economic costs, but scheduling the holiday to coincide with existing state/federal calendars can mitigate disruption.</li><li>Broad bipartisan public support exists, especially among younger generations.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Federal Legislation</h3>
<ul><li>Pass the Youth Voting Access and Civic Recognition Act to declare federal Election Day a national public holiday.</li></ul>
<h3>Local and State Incentives</h3>
<ul><li>Offer federal grants to states and school districts that pair the holiday with civic learning initiatives.</li></ul>
<h3>Private Sector Engagement</h3>
<ul><li>Incentivize businesses through tax credits to provide paid time off if they remain open on Election Day.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Voting should not require sacrifice—especially for young Americans trying to make their voices heard. A national Voting Holiday affirms the value of civic participation and lowers turnout barriers for the next generation of voters.</p>
