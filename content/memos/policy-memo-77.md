<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Older migrants often face fragmented pension entitlements when crossing borders, leaving them economically vulnerable in later life. This memo proposes that UNDESA facilitate bilateral and multilateral agreements ensuring pension portability, especially for workers in the Global South who return to their countries of origin without access to the retirement benefits they earned abroad.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>The number of older international migrants surpassed 35 million in 2023.</li><li>Many migrants retire in countries different from where they accrued pension rights.</li><li>Existing pension systems rarely accommodate transnational employment histories.</li><li>Informal and undocumented workers are disproportionately excluded from benefits.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>The current lack of pension portability creates significant financial insecurity among aging populations:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Jurisdictional Gaps:</strong> National systems are often not aligned to track or honor contributions made abroad.</li><li><strong>Documentation Barriers:</strong> Many older migrants cannot access their pension entitlements due to lost records or undocumented work.</li><li><strong>Gender Disparities:</strong> Female migrants often engage in informal caregiving, which is excluded from most pension schemes.</li><li><strong>Fiscal Strain:</strong> Host and origin countries both face challenges in allocating resources for non-resident retirees without coordinated systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>International Pension Frameworks</h3>
<ul><li>Negotiate bilateral and regional portability agreements modeled after EU and Ibero-American systems.</li><li>Create a UNDESA-led global registry for cross-border pension contributions.</li></ul>
<h3>Inclusion of Informal Work</h3>
<ul><li>Promote recognition of informal caregiving and domestic work in pension eligibility.</li><li>Encourage governments to subsidize contributions for low-income migrant workers.</li></ul>
<h3>Legal and Documentation Access</h3>
<ul><li>Fund legal clinics and digital tools for pension documentation recovery.</li><li>Standardize ID and work documentation across borders with aging populations in mind.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Ensuring pension portability is not only a question of economic justice, but of human dignity in aging. UNDESA can lead the global effort to design transnational retirement protections that reflect modern migration realities.</p>
