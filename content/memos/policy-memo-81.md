<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Older adults are frequently overlooked in humanitarian responses to displacement, despite heightened vulnerability. This memo urges UNHRC to mainstream elder inclusion into refugee protection frameworks, disaster response, and IDP guidelines.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 7 million displaced people are over the age of 60.</li><li>Humanitarian response plans often neglect elder-specific needs.</li><li>Mobility, health, and social care for older refugees are chronically underfunded.</li><li>Statelessness among elders leads to denial of aid and services.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Displaced elders face compounded challenges:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Access Barriers:</strong> Older people may be unable to travel to aid centers or navigate complex registration systems.</li><li><strong>Health Risks:</strong> Chronic conditions worsen without continuous care or medication.</li><li><strong>Exclusion from Planning:</strong> Elders are rarely included in needs assessments or advisory councils.</li><li><strong>Documentation Gaps:</strong> Many older refugees lack legal papers, excluding them from services.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Elder-Inclusive Humanitarian Planning</h3>
<ul><li>Require age-disaggregated data in all refugee response plans.</li><li>Include older adults in camp design, food distribution, and healthcare provision.</li></ul>
<h3>Legal Documentation Access</h3>
<ul><li>Fund mobile registration and identity restoration programs for older refugees.</li><li>Offer age-friendly legal support for stateless elders.</li></ul>
<h3>Healthcare Continuity</h3>
<ul><li>Ensure provision of chronic illness medications and assistive devices.</li><li>Train humanitarian workers in geriatric care.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Older displaced persons are often invisible in crisis response. The UNHRC must lead in recognizing their rights and ensuring age equity in global humanitarian frameworks.</p>
