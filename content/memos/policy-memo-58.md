<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>LGBTQ+ youth, particularly in hostile environments, are overrepresented in informal and gig economies—often as a survival strategy. This memo highlights the intersection of queer precarity and labor insecurity and urges the ILO to develop labor rights frameworks inclusive of gender and sexual minorities in informal work, focusing on protections, non-discrimination, and economic inclusion.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Queer youth face disproportionate unemployment due to family rejection, educational exclusion, and workplace bias. As a result, many turn to informal or gig work—such as street vending, freelance delivery, or digital content creation—where labor protections are weak or nonexistent. This often leaves them vulnerable to exploitation, harassment, and financial precarity, with little legal recourse.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Discrimination in Hiring:</strong> LGBTQ+ youth face direct exclusion from formal employment markets.</li><li><strong>Platform Exploitation:</strong> Gig platforms often lack anti-harassment policies or mechanisms to protect queer workers from customer abuse.</li><li><strong>Housing and Income Insecurity:</strong> Without formal employment, queer youth struggle to secure housing, healthcare, or credit.</li><li><strong>Criminalization:</strong> In some countries, informal queer work—particularly online—is targeted as “immorality,” leading to arrests or surveillance.</li><li><strong>Mental and Physical Risks:</strong> Gig work often involves unsafe conditions, long hours, and no access to benefits or reporting channels.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Inclusive Labor Policies</h3>
<ul><li>Require gig platforms to adopt anti-discrimination and harassment safeguards for queer workers.</li><li>Extend labor protections and benefits to informal youth workers, with specific protections for LGBTQ+ identities.</li></ul>
<h3>Economic Inclusion Programs</h3>
<ul><li>Support entrepreneurship programs and microgrants for LGBTQ+ youth-led informal businesses.</li><li>Develop queer-inclusive workforce training and certification pathways.</li></ul>
<h3>Legal Recognition and Documentation</h3>
<ul><li>Ensure informal workers can register businesses and access ID/legal documents without gender or sexuality-based barriers.</li><li>Push for decriminalization of queer-associated informal work.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitoring and Advocacy</h3>
<ul><li>Fund participatory research on LGBTQ+ youth in the informal economy.</li><li>Include queer youth labor protections in the ILO’s Decent Work Agenda.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Precarity is not just an economic issue—it is a queer justice issue. The ILO must extend protections to LGBTQ+ youth in informal work and ensure that economic survival does not come at the cost of dignity, safety, or rights.</p>
