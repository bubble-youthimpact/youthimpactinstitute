<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Schools and juvenile justice programs are adopting AI emotion recognition to assess “threat levels” or detect dishonesty in youth. These tools often misread neurodivergent behavior and reinforce racial bias. UNESCO must act to ban emotion AI in disciplinary contexts involving minors.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Emotion recognition software claims to infer mental states from facial expressions, tone, and micro-movements. Used in classrooms, courts, and social work, it scores youth as “agitated,” “aggressive,” or “untrustworthy.” But scientific consensus disputes its validity—especially across cultures, neurotypes, and trauma profiles. Such tools often misclassify autistic stimming, Black facial affect, or trauma responses as dangerous.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Scientific Dubiousness:</strong> Emotion is not universally legible or inferable by machines.</li><li><strong>Cultural and Neurodivergent Misreadings:</strong> Systems penalize behavior outside normative affect.</li><li><strong>Surveillance in Disguise:</strong> Framed as safety tools, these systems are de facto lie detectors.</li><li><strong>Dehumanization:</strong> Youth become objects of biometric suspicion, not participants in care.</li></ul>
<p>Emotion AI turns youth discipline into digital phrenology—unscientific, biased, and punitive.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>UNESCO should declare emotion AI in child disciplinary systems a rights violation.</li><li>Ban its use in schools, courts, and detention centers involving minors.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Require full disclosure of use and logic behind any emotion-based scoring.</li><li>Offer public legal pathways to challenge misclassification.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Promote moratoria on pseudoscientific AI tools lacking peer-reviewed validation.</li><li>Regulate vendors marketing emotion detection to child-serving institutions.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Support youth campaigns to expose emotion AI in schools and juvenile justice.</li><li>Fund non-carceral alternatives to youth behavioral support systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Machines cannot feel—and they shouldn’t decide how we discipline those who do. UNESCO must champion youth dignity over digital profiling and stop the spread of emotion AI in systems of punishment.</p>
