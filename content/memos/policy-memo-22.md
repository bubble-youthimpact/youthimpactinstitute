<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Artificial Intelligence is transforming education, employment, and civic participation—but AI access remains profoundly unequal. Youth in the Global South are often consumers, not co-creators, of AI technologies. This memo calls for UNESCO to promote equitable access to AI tools, training, and infrastructure for youth in under-resourced regions, closing the emerging global “AI divide.”</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>While global investment in AI skyrockets, access to the technology and its development remains highly concentrated in the Global North.<br>Key disparities:</p>
<ul><li>Over 70% of AI research and development funding is in North America and Europe.</li><li>Youth in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia report limited exposure to AI education or tools.</li><li>OpenAI models and cloud-based tools are often inaccessible due to bandwidth and cost.</li></ul>
<p>This inequality replicates existing power dynamics: those with AI access shape policy, economy, and culture, while others are left behind or subjected to AI systems they did not help design.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Systemic barriers include:<br>Infrastructure Gaps: Limited internet speed, electricity, and device access constrain AI tool deployment.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Skills Shortage:</strong> Lack of local AI educators, mentors, and relevant language content.</li><li><strong>Cultural Exclusion:</strong> Most AI tools are trained on data from high-income, Western contexts.</li><li><strong>Dependency Models:</strong> Youth in the Global South consume but cannot meaningfully adapt or challenge imported AI tools.</li></ul>
<p>Without intervention, this divide could deepen global inequality and silence local voices in technological innovation.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>AI Infrastructure Investment</h3>
<ul><li>Support regional AI hubs with internet, electricity, and shared hardware.</li><li>Promote mobile-first, low-bandwidth AI education tools.</li></ul>
<h3>Localized AI Education</h3>
<ul><li>Develop culturally relevant AI curricula in local languages.</li><li>Train teachers and youth leaders in foundational AI concepts and ethics.</li></ul>
<h3>Open Access and Open Source</h3>
<ul><li>Fund open-source AI projects developed by and for communities in the Global South.</li><li>Negotiate subsidized cloud access for schools and community centers.</li></ul>
<h3>South-South Collaboration</h3>
<ul><li>Facilitate AI knowledge exchange between countries in the Global South.</li><li>Create fellowships and innovation prizes for youth-led AI projects.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>AI must serve all humanity, not just those who already hold technological power. UNESCO can bridge the AI divide by fostering inclusive, locally grounded pathways for youth to learn, build, and lead in the AI era.</p>
