<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Facial recognition is being introduced in schools to automate attendance and monitor student behavior. This surveillance threatens student privacy, exacerbates racial bias, and creates a carceral atmosphere. UNESCO must call for a ban on biometric surveillance in education.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Schools in China, the U.S., India, and Brazil now use facial recognition to log attendance, detect “disruptive” behavior, or flag unrecognized visitors. Some claim to track emotional engagement or attention span. Yet studies show these systems perform worst on students with darker skin, hijabs, or non-normative appearances. Facial surveillance normalizes panoptic control in learning spaces, turning education into monitoring.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Normalization of Surveillance:</strong> Youth grow up expecting to be watched constantly.</li><li><strong>Biased Accuracy:</strong> Marginalized students are more likely to be misidentified.</li><li><strong>Consent Illusion:</strong> Facial data is often captured without meaningful opt-out.</li><li><strong>Psychological Harm:</strong> Surveillance chills speech, curiosity, and freedom of expression.</li></ul>
<p>Schools should be spaces of trust and learning—not biometric control.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>UNESCO should call for a global moratorium on facial recognition in educational spaces.</li><li>Classify student biometric data as sensitive and protected.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Require schools to disclose all surveillance tools and provide opt-out mechanisms.</li><li>Mandate deletion of biometric data after limited retention periods.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Ban sales of facial recognition to K-12 institutions.</li><li>Require independent testing of all school surveillance tech for bias and harm.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Fund student-led privacy campaigns in schools.</li><li>Support development of tech-free models for attendance and safety.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Education is not compatible with constant facial surveillance. UNESCO must protect youth autonomy and dignity from biometric overreach in schools, ensuring classrooms remain spaces of inquiry—not control.</p>
