<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Youth mental health is in crisis. Despite increased awareness, school attendance policies still penalize students for taking time off due to psychological distress. This memo proposes a federal mandate requiring all public schools to formally recognize mental health days as excused absences, removing barriers to care and reducing stigma. Doing so would advance both educational equity and youth wellbeing.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>The U.S. Surgeon General declared youth mental health a national emergency in 2021. Many states still lack policies allowing students to take excused absences for mental health reasons. Data shows that untreated stress, anxiety, and depression negatively impact academic performance, attendance, and graduation rates. Youth of color, LGBTQ+ students, and low-income students face disproportionate mental health burdens, often compounded by punitive school discipline.<br>Additional context:</p>
<ul><li>Only 13 states explicitly allow mental health days as excused absences.</li><li>School culture often prioritizes “resilience” over recovery, leading students to conceal symptoms.</li><li>Mental health crises often go untreated until they escalate into disciplinary or medical emergencies.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Denying youth the right to take care of their mental health effectively criminalizes vulnerability.</li><li>Universal excused mental health leave would promote early intervention and reduce long-term absences.</li><li>This policy aligns with a rights-based understanding of education that centers student health and dignity.</li><li>School leaders worry about abuse of the system—but data from states with these laws shows minimal misuse and improved attendance in the long run.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Federal Mental Health Absence Standardization</h3>
<ul><li>Require that all public K–12 schools permit a minimum number of annual excused mental health days.</li><li>Protect students from grade penalties or disciplinary action for using these days.</li></ul>
<h3>School Communication and Training</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate that schools inform students and families about this right through handbooks and orientation.</li><li>Train educators and attendance officers on recognizing and supporting mental health needs.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitoring and Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Require schools to report mental health-related absences and referrals as part of federal educational equity data.</li><li>Evaluate mental health leave impacts annually through student feedback and attendance patterns.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Mental health care must be treated as a fundamental part of the right to education. By embedding mental health days in federal attendance policy, the Department of Education can reduce stigma, support academic success, and promote youth wellbeing across the country.</p>
