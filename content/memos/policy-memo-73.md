<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>As climate change displaces millions, youth are emerging as a particularly vulnerable group among environmental migrants. Yet current international legal frameworks fail to recognize climate-displaced youth as protected under refugee or human rights law. This memo urges the UNFCCC to advance a new legal recognition of “climate youth migrants,” with tailored protections rooted in intergenerational and environmental justice.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Climate change is reshaping human mobility patterns globally:</p>
<ul><li>43.1 million people were internally displaced by climate-related disasters in the last five years (IDMC, 2024).</li><li>Youth aged 15–24 make up a significant portion of those displaced, particularly in island nations and rural agrarian communities.</li><li>Climate migrants often fall outside the scope of the 1951 Refugee Convention and receive no formal status.</li><li>Without legal status, displaced youth face barriers to education, work, and protection.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>The invisibility of climate youth migrants exposes policy gaps:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Legal Limbo:</strong> Youth displaced by climate change are often labeled as “voluntary” migrants, undermining their rights.</li><li><strong>Disrupted Development:</strong> Climate migration interrupts education, family structures, and identity formation.</li><li><strong>Colonial Legacies:</strong> Youth in the Global South are disproportionately affected, despite contributing least to climate change.</li><li><strong>Lack of Youth Voice:</strong> Young people are excluded from migration and climate policy processes that determine their futures.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legal Recognition and Rights Framework</h3>
<ul><li>Call for a UNFCCC-led international declaration recognizing youth climate migrants as a protected category.</li><li>Integrate this definition into regional compacts (e.g., the Cartagena Declaration, Kampala Convention).</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Focused Climate Mobility Protocols</h3>
<ul><li>Fund legal and humanitarian pathways for youth displaced by environmental collapse.</li><li>Mandate access to education, documentation, and psychosocial support during resettlement.</li></ul>
<h3>Participation and Advocacy</h3>
<ul><li>Include youth climate migrants in COP delegations, migration forums, and treaty negotiations.</li><li>Fund youth-led climate-migration advocacy organizations.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Young people displaced by climate disasters are among the most vulnerable—and the most ignored. Recognizing them under international law is a moral and political imperative. The UNFCCC must act now to protect the climate generation from both environmental and legal precarity.</p>
