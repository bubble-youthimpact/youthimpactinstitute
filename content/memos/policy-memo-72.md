<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Thousands of young migrants are deported annually due to minor infractions, entrenching the global “crimmigration” crisis—the merging of criminal and immigration systems. This memo calls on the IOM to address the disproportionate targeting of migrant youth for deportation based on low-level offenses or racial profiling. Reform should prioritize decarceration, due process, and youth reintegration rather than expulsion.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Criminalization of migrant youth leads to unjust removals:</p>
<ul><li>In many countries, noncitizen youth can be deported for infractions, such as shoplifting, curfew violations, or marijuana possession.</li><li>Youth of color, especially Black, Afro-descendant, and Indigenous migrants, are overpoliced and overrepresented in deportation pipelines.</li><li>Many are deported to countries they barely know or where they face violence, stigma, or state neglect.</li><li>Immigration judges often lack discretion to consider youth age, rehabilitation, or family ties.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Youth deportation for petty crimes reflects systemic injustice:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Overlapping Systems:</strong> Youth are punished twice—first by juvenile justice, then immigration enforcement.</li><li><strong>No Second Chances:</strong> Legal frameworks often exclude young migrants from rehabilitation pathways.</li><li><strong>Family Separation: </strong>Deportation breaks up families and destabilizes entire communities.</li><li><strong>Chilling Effect:</strong> Fear of deportation deters migrant youth from reporting crimes or seeking help.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>End Crimmigration for Youth</h3>
<ul><li>Advocate for IOM support of legal reforms that prohibit deportation based solely on minor offenses.</li><li>Call for the decriminalization of youth migration and the decoupling of juvenile justice from immigration enforcement.</li></ul>
<h3>Rehabilitation and Reintegration, Not Removal</h3>
<ul><li>Promote community-based restorative justice models and diversion programs for migrant youth.</li><li>Support binational reintegration programs only when returns are truly voluntary and safe.</li></ul>
<h3>Data, Oversight, and Transparency</h3>
<ul><li>Launch a global youth deportation data initiative to monitor disproportionate impacts.</li><li>Fund legal aid and advocacy centers that support youth facing removal.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Criminalizing and deporting youth for survival-based or low-level offenses is not justice—it is a form of racialized expulsion. The IOM must lead efforts to reimagine migrant youth as agents of renewal, not targets of exile.</p>
