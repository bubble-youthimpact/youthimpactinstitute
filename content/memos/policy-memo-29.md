<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>In today’s world, digital access is vital for education, communication, and reintegration. Yet youth in prison are systematically excluded from the digital sphere. This memo calls on UNESCO to define digital connectivity as a human right for incarcerated minors and to lead a global push for safe, educational internet access in juvenile justice systems.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Globally, over 1 million youth are incarcerated, most without digital access.<br>Key issues include:</p>
<ul><li>Barriers to remote learning, especially during crises.</li><li>Lack of access to legal resources, vocational training, or contact with family.</li><li>Reintegration difficulties due to digital illiteracy.</li></ul>
<p>This exclusion deepens inequality and undermines rehabilitation.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Core problems include:<br>Security Concerns: Governments fear misuse, but over-restrict access.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Outdated Infrastructure:</strong> Many detention centers lack basic digital tools.</li><li><strong>Policy Gaps:</strong> Few global standards exist for digital rights of prisoners.</li><li><strong>Reentry Risk:</strong> Youth are released into tech-dependent societies they can't navigate.</li></ul>
<p>A new framework is needed that balances safety with digital empowerment.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Access and Tools</h3>
<ul><li>Promote the provision of filtered educational internet in youth facilities.</li><li>Support the distribution of secure digital devices for learning and legal access.</li></ul>
<h3>Digital Literacy</h3>
<ul><li>Fund tech education programs for incarcerated youth.</li><li>Include coding, critical media literacy, and internet safety.</li></ul>
<h3>Policy and Guidelines</h3>
<ul><li>Draft international standards for digital rights in incarceration.</li><li>Encourage governments to adopt rehabilitative digital policies.</li></ul>
<h3>Reentry Programs</h3>
<ul><li>Build reentry tech boot camps for digital reintegration.</li><li>Connect youth with mentors and job platforms online pre-release.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>In the digital age, disconnection is a sentence beyond incarceration. By championing digital rights for youth in prison, UNESCO can ensure technology becomes a tool of liberation, not exclusion.</p>
