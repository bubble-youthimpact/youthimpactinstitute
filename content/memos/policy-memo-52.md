<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Despite global condemnation, conversion therapy remains legal or unregulated in over 100 countries. Queer youth are disproportionately targeted, often by family members, religious institutions, or state-sponsored clinics. This memo urges OHCHR to spearhead a global ban on conversion practices by framing them as a youth-specific form of torture and launching protective policy mechanisms across regions.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Conversion therapy refers to a range of pseudoscientific practices aimed at changing or suppressing an individual’s sexual orientation or gender identity. These practices, often framed as "healing" or "reform," can include psychological coercion, aversive techniques, and spiritual abuse. UN bodies including the Independent Expert on SOGI have recognized these as degrading and harmful. Still, youth continue to be subjected to them due to parental consent loopholes and medical complicity.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Medical Gatekeeping:</strong> Licensed professionals in mental health and medicine sometimes participate in conversion, shielded by vague "therapeutic exception" clauses.</li><li><strong>Religious Cover:</strong> Faith-based actors present conversion therapy as spiritual care, making regulation difficult in religiously conservative states.</li><li><strong>Youth Vulnerability:</strong> Minors cannot legally consent to such treatment, yet laws often allow parental override of youth autonomy.</li><li><strong>Legal Silence:</strong> Many countries have no laws banning these practices, and in others, bans are weakly enforced or limited to narrow age ranges.</li><li><strong>Cross-Border Risk:</strong> Youth are trafficked across borders to attend conversion camps or religious exorcisms, evading domestic jurisdiction.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legal Reform</h3>
<ul><li>Advocate for international treaties explicitly defining conversion practices as a form of cruel, inhuman, and degrading treatment.</li><li>Push national governments to criminalize conversion therapy for all age groups, with enhanced penalties for youth-targeted cases.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Protections</h3>
<ul><li>Establish international safe reporting platforms for victims of conversion practices.</li><li>Support youth shelters and affirming mental health care in countries where bans do not yet exist.</li></ul>
<h3>Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Pressure professional associations to revoke licenses of practitioners engaging in conversion therapy.</li><li>Partner with interfaith coalitions to develop accountability protocols for religious institutions.</li></ul>
<h3>Survivor Leadership</h3>
<p>Fund survivor-led campaigns and oral history archives to shift public narratives.<br>Include queer youth survivors in international human rights review processes.</p>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Conversion therapy is not a medical practice—it is systemic psychological violence. Protecting queer youth demands that we treat it not as a cultural issue but as a global human rights crisis requiring coordinated legal, cultural, and survivor-led responses.</p>
