<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Elder abuse—physical, psychological, financial, and institutional—affects 1 in 6 older adults globally, often within health systems meant to protect them. This memo proposes WHO lead a rights-based framework for recognizing, reporting, and preventing elder abuse in medical and caregiving environments.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>WHO estimates that nearly 16% of people aged 60+ experience abuse each year.</li><li>Institutional abuse in care homes often goes unreported due to fear or cognitive decline.</li><li>Workforce shortages and poor training exacerbate abusive dynamics.</li><li>Older adults in marginalized communities face compounding risks (e.g. racism, poverty).</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Health systems often fail to recognize or intervene in elder abuse due to:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Low Reporting Rates:</strong> Abuse is underreported due to stigma and lack of institutional accountability.</li><li><strong>Inadequate Training:</strong> Health professionals often lack gerontological education and abuse detection tools.</li><li><strong>Structural Neglect:</strong> Understaffing and austerity in long-term care create environments ripe for abuse.</li><li><strong>Legal Loopholes:</strong> Many countries lack laws explicitly protecting elders in institutional settings.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Global Reporting Protocols</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate elder abuse screening in all WHO member health systems.</li><li>Standardize data collection across hospitals, nursing homes, and home care.</li></ul>
<h3>Workforce Reform</h3>
<ul><li>Require geriatric and human rights training for all care staff.</li><li>Incentivize better pay and working conditions to reduce burnout.</li></ul>
<h3>Legal Protection Measures</h3>
<ul><li>Help governments adopt elder-specific protection laws.</li><li>Fund confidential abuse hotlines and ombudsman programs.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Elder abuse within care systems is a hidden human rights crisis. WHO must assert leadership in ensuring global norms, protections, and accountability mechanisms for older people in health institutions.</p>
