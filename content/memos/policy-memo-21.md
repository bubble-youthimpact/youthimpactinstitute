<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Young people are among the most active users of digital platforms, yet they are often unaware of the extent to which their personal data is harvested, analyzed, and monetized. This memo proposes a rights-based regulatory framework to protect youth from data exploitation, enhance transparency, and build digital trust through education and enforcement.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>From social media and online learning to games and mental health apps, youth today live much of their lives online. However, most platforms are built on surveillance-based business models that extract behavioral data—including location, browsing habits, emotional responses, and social networks—for targeted advertising or sale to third parties.<br>Key concerns include:</p>
<ul><li>Children’s apps that track usage beyond educational purposes.</li><li>Social platforms profiling teens to optimize content and increase screen time.</li><li>Health and wellness apps sharing sensitive data without consent.</li></ul>
<p>Current data protection regimes, such as the GDPR or COPPA, often exclude adolescents or are poorly enforced. The result is a digital environment where youth are both overexposed and underprotected.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Major issues:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Opaque Consent:</strong> Privacy policies are too complex for youth to understand, and consent mechanisms are often meaningless.</li><li><strong>Behavioral Manipulation:</strong> Data-driven recommendation systems shape emotions and decisions without awareness or control.</li><li><strong>Data Monetization:</strong> Youth data is a profitable commodity in a largely unregulated marketplace.</li><li><strong>Legal Gaps:</strong> Most national laws do not treat adolescent data with the same protections as children's data.</li></ul>
<p>This asymmetry of power undermines autonomy, exposes youth to commercial manipulation, and erodes digital trust.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Global Youth Data Charter</h3>
<ul><li>Develop an international charter establishing youth rights over their personal data.</li><li>Include provisions for meaningful consent, data minimization, and ethical use.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Require age-appropriate privacy policies and consent prompts.</li><li>Mandate impact assessments for platforms targeting youth.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Education and Agency</h3>
<ul><li>Integrate data literacy into digital citizenship curricula.</li><li>Fund youth-led campaigns on digital rights and surveillance awareness.</li></ul>
<h3>Enforcement and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Support member states in strengthening privacy enforcement bodies.</li><li>Create transnational mechanisms for youth to file data-related grievances.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Youth deserve digital spaces that respect their autonomy and protect their rights. UNESCO can lead by codifying global standards, educating young users, and holding digital platforms accountable for the ethical treatment of youth data.</p>
