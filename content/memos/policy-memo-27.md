<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Girls and young women are an overlooked population in prison reform, often subjected to abuse, neglect, and re-traumatization. This memo urges UNESCO to champion gender-responsive reforms that address the unique needs of incarcerated girls, from reproductive care to trauma-informed mental health services.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>While boys represent the majority of youth in detention, the number of incarcerated girls is rising, especially for status offenses (e.g., running away, truancy).<br>Key facts:</p>
<ul><li>Over 80% of incarcerated girls have a history of abuse or neglect.</li><li>Many are pregnant or parenting at the time of detention.</li><li>Facilities are ill-equipped to support their physical and emotional needs.</li></ul>
<p>Incarcerated girls face higher risks of sexual violence, inadequate menstrual care, and long-term psychological harm.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Key concerns:<br>Gendered Pathways to Incarceration: Girls are often criminalized for trauma responses.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Lack of Services:</strong> Few detention centers provide appropriate gynecological or maternal care.</li><li><strong>Over-Surveillance:</strong> Behavioral infractions are disproportionately punished in girls' facilities.</li><li><strong>Post-Release Vulnerability:</strong> Reentry services are rarely tailored to gendered risks, such as exploitation or homelessness.</li></ul>
<p>Reform must address not just legal injustice, but the lived realities of incarcerated girls.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Gender-Sensitive Facility Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Promote the design of detention centers that ensure dignity, privacy, and care for girls.</li><li>Require access to menstrual hygiene, reproductive health, and gender-specific counseling.</li></ul>
<h3>Trauma-Informed Programming</h3>
<ul><li>Train staff in gender-responsive, non-punitive behavioral management.</li><li>Support peer mentorship and arts-based healing programs for girls.</li></ul>
<h3>Alternatives to Incarceration</h3>
<ul><li>Advocate for diversion programs that keep girls out of detention for non-violent offenses.</li><li>Expand access to community-based services for at-risk girls.</li></ul>
<h3>Global Monitoring and Advocacy</h3>
<ul><li>Track incarceration trends by gender and age in all UNESCO member states.</li><li>Support campaigns to raise awareness of girls’ justice issues globally.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Girls behind bars face multiple layers of invisibility and harm. UNESCO has the platform to shift the global justice paradigm toward one that recognizes and protects their unique rights, stories, and futures.</p>
