<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Remote learning platforms increasingly rely on AI proctoring and behavior analytics to monitor students, often collecting biometric and behavioral data without meaningful consent. These systems disproportionately affect marginalized youth and erode educational trust. UNESCO must intervene to uphold youth digital privacy in education.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Since the COVID-19 pandemic, remote learning has become a permanent fixture in global education. Platforms, such as Proctorio and Examity, use facial recognition, keystroke analysis, and screen tracking to detect “suspicious” behavior. However, these AI tools often flag neurodivergent behavior, non-standard test environments, or even dark skin tones as threats. Students in rural, disabled, or low-income communities—who are more likely to share spaces or lack high-speed internet—are disproportionately penalized. Many are unaware of how their data is stored, sold, or repurposed.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Surveillance Creep: What began as cheating prevention now enables full-time behavioral tracking.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Consent Failure:</strong> Students rarely have the option to opt out of data collection.</li><li><strong>Algorithmic Discrimination:</strong> False positives target marginalized students, exacerbating inequality.</li><li><strong>Commercial Exploitation:</strong> Student data is increasingly monetized by edtech vendors without accountability.</li></ul>
<p>AI-based surveillance in education threatens both learning outcomes and fundamental rights. Privacy must be non-negotiable.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>UNESCO should establish a Student Data Privacy Charter rooted in child rights.</li><li>Require human oversight in AI-based disciplinary actions or exam flagging.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate disclosure of surveillance data collection practices by edtech companies.</li><li>Create school-based digital rights advocates and appeals mechanisms for flagged students.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Advocate for edtech regulations that limit biometric tracking and ban emotion detection.</li><li>Push for open-source auditability of AI used in learning environments.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Fund student-led digital ethics initiatives in education.</li><li>Support alternative privacy-first learning platforms developed with youth input.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Learning should not feel like being watched. UNESCO must ensure that education technologies respect students’ dignity, consent, and cognitive liberty. Remote learning can be inclusive—but only if it is also just.</p>
