<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Globally, millions of girls miss school due to water scarcity, sanitation barriers, and menstruation stigma. Water insecurity is an underrecognized driver of gender inequality in education. UNESCO should lead intersectoral efforts to integrate water access, menstrual equity, and gender-sensitive infrastructure into education policy frameworks.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>In sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia, the burden of water collection falls disproportionately on girls, with some walking hours each day. Lack of sanitation facilities in schools also deters attendance—especially during menstruation. Despite decades of education access initiatives, the physical realities of water insecurity continue to be sidelined in gender equity discourse.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Opportunity Costs: Time spent collecting water reduces school attendance and performance.<br>Dropout Risk: Inadequate menstrual hygiene facilities drive girls to drop out early.<br>Public Health Intersections: Lack of clean water increases disease risk, affecting cognitive development.<br>Policy Silos: Water, health, and education ministries often operate without coordination.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Integrated Infrastructure Investment</h3>
<ul><li>Co-fund water points and gender-segregated latrines at schools.</li><li>Prioritize schools in high water-scarcity regions for WASH (Water, Sanitation and Hygiene) interventions.</li></ul>
<h3>Menstrual Equity Initiatives</h3>
<ul><li>Fund free menstrual product distribution in schools.</li><li>Include menstrual health education in national curricula.</li></ul>
<h3>Cross-Sector Coordination</h3>
<ul><li>Convene interministerial task forces on water and education equity.</li><li>Incorporate water access metrics into UNESCO’s education access reports.</li></ul>
<h3>Community Engagement</h3>
<ul><li>Train girls as WASH ambassadors and local leaders.</li><li>Collaborate with local NGOs on culturally responsive water and hygiene campaigns.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Water insecurity is not just a health crisis—it is a barrier to girls’ futures. By embedding water access and menstrual dignity into its education mandate, UNESCO can help dismantle systemic barriers and enable every girl to attend, stay, and thrive in school.</p>
