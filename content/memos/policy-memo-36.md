<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>From facial recognition to predictive behavior monitoring, AI surveillance technologies are increasingly used in schools—often without consent or oversight. These tools disproportionately target marginalized students and risk infringing on human rights. UNESCO must establish global guidelines to ensure education remains a space of trust, not surveillance.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>AI-driven surveillance in schools includes biometric ID scanners, emotion detection software, and algorithms that flag “at-risk” behavior. Though often justified as tools for safety or academic performance, they raise critical concerns about data privacy, consent, and algorithmic bias. In China, facial recognition monitors attention in classrooms; in the U.S., predictive policing software flags students for intervention—often correlating with race and disability.</p>
<p>Analysis<br>Major concerns include:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Lack of Regulation:</strong> Few countries have clear laws governing school-based surveillance.</li><li><strong>Algorithmic Bias:</strong> AI tools often reproduce discriminatory patterns, disproportionately flagging students of color.</li><li><strong>Psychological Harm:</strong> Constant surveillance can inhibit learning, autonomy, and free expression.</li><li><strong>Opaque Data Use:</strong> Students and parents are often unaware of how data is collected, used, or shared.</li></ul>
<p>UNESCO must ensure schools do not become testing grounds for unchecked AI.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Global Guidelines</h3>
<ul><li>Develop a UNESCO charter on ethical AI use in education.</li><li>Prohibit biometric surveillance without informed consent.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate disclosures about data collection and algorithmic decision-making.</li><li>Require third-party audits for fairness, accuracy, and bias.</li></ul>
<h3>Alternatives to Surveillance</h3>
<ul><li>Promote trust-building, restorative approaches to school safety.</li><li>Fund privacy-respecting digital tools for student support.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice</h3>
<ul><li>Ensure students are consulted in school tech policy decisions.</li><li>Create youth privacy councils within education ministries.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Education should liberate, not monitor. UNESCO must protect student dignity by shaping ethical standards for AI in learning environments worldwide.</p>
