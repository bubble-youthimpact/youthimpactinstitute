<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The Democratic Party has long prided itself on representing the will of the people, but internal governance structures often reflect top-down control rather than grassroots participation. As the 2026 midterms approach, trust in party mechanisms is fraying—particularly among progressive youth activists, union members, and community organizers. This memo proposes a series of reforms to empower grassroots delegates in DNC governance and convention proceedings. Increasing transparency, decentralizing decision-making, and strengthening delegate autonomy are critical for building a party that models the democracy it seeks to defend.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Grassroots organizers played a pivotal role in Democratic victories in Georgia (2021), Pennsylvania (2022), and Wisconsin (2023).</li><li>Many state party conventions limit delegate participation to well-connected insiders or large donors.</li><li>DNC rules and platform committees remain dominated by appointees with ties to national leadership.</li><li>Youth, BIPOC, and rural voices are often underrepresented in delegate bodies and procedural debates.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>The Democratic Party's legitimacy relies on bottom-up mobilization, yet its structure often replicates elite gatekeeping. Delegate rules and procedures that favor incumbents can dampen enthusiasm and participation. Without reform, the DNC risks alienating its most effective organizing base. Empowering a new generation of delegates—especially from historically excluded constituencies—would revitalize internal democracy and produce platforms that reflect the party’s diverse coalition.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Decentralized Delegate Selection</h3>
<ul><li>Expand open selection processes for delegates at state and district levels.</li><li>Cap donor thresholds in delegate eligibility to limit plutocratic overrepresentation.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Committee Reform</h3>
<ul><li>Require proportional youth representation (at least 25%) in platform drafting bodies.</li><li>Include community-based organizations in selecting platform committee members.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Accessibility</h3>
<ul><li>Livestream all delegate rule debates and credential challenges.</li><li>Provide multilingual and disability-accessible materials for delegate participation.</li></ul>
<h3>Empowerment Training</h3>
<ul><li>Launch a DNC-wide Delegate Democracy Academy for young, rural, and first-time delegates.</li><li>Fund civic education programs on party governance in high schools and colleges.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>True democratic leadership begins with a democratic party. The DNC must recommit to participatory governance that empowers rather than manages its base. Rebuilding internal trust and amplifying grassroots leadership will yield not only a stronger party platform but also a reinvigorated electorate ready to defend democracy in 2026 and beyond.</p>
