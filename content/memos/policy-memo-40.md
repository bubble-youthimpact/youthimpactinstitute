<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>AI systems are increasingly used to match refugees to resettlement countries, services, and education—but they often exclude or misallocate youth based on biased data. UNESCO must ensure refugee youth are not reduced to algorithmic profiles in systems that determine their futures.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>The UNHCR and partner governments now use algorithmic decision tools to rank and sort refugee applicants. Factors, such as “cultural fit,” “language acquisition potential,” or “economic viability,” are coded into opaque models that ignore the lived complexity of displaced youth. These tools often lack input from affected populations, ignore trauma, and fail to account for the needs of minors. Errors or poor placements lead to educational disruption, social isolation, or re-traumatization.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>One-Size-Fits-All Logic: AI systems often generalize based on reductive categories.<br>Unaccountable Placements: Youth often don’t understand or have control over their assignments.<br>Techno-Bureaucratic Drift: Responsibility is shifted to the algorithm, insulating agencies from critique.<br>Rights Violations: Children’s best interests are sidelined for “optimization.”<br>We cannot allow refugee youth to be treated as data points in a resettlement formula.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>UNESCO should develop youth-first resettlement principles requiring participatory algorithm design.</li><li>Require human interviews and caseworker override for all youth assignments.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate explainability standards for refugee decision systems.</li><li>Create multilingual feedback platforms for refugee youth to report and appeal errors.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Require audits for all AI tools used in humanitarian services.</li><li>Prohibit algorithmic profiling based on race, religion, or political activity.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Fund refugee youth councils to co-design resettlement policies.</li><li>Support storytelling platforms that amplify youth refugee experiences with resettlement systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Youth resettlement is not a technical problem—it is a human one. UNESCO must ensure that AI tools serve young refugees as individuals with needs and rights, not simply variables in a global optimization model.</p>
