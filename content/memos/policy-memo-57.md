<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>In several countries, girls and young women are subjected to forced virginity testing or are required to present “chastity certificates” to attend school or participate in extracurricular activities. These practices constitute a form of sexual violence and discrimination. This memo urges UNFPA to denounce these practices and support governments in banning virginity testing, reforming school health policies, and promoting comprehensive, consent-based sexual education.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Virginity testing—a medically unscientific examination to determine if a girl or woman has had vaginal intercourse—has been condemned by the WHO, UN Women, and OHCHR as a violation of human rights. Still, it is practiced in over 20 countries across Africa, Asia, and Eastern Europe, often under the guise of tradition or morality enforcement. In school systems, these practices disproportionately target adolescent girls, violate bodily autonomy, and reinforce patriarchal control over female sexuality.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Medical Inaccuracy:</strong> The “two-finger test” and hymen examinations are unreliable and have no basis in science.</li><li><strong>School Dropouts and Stigma:</strong> Girls who fail virginity tests or refuse to undergo them face expulsion, public shaming, and lifelong stigma.</li><li><strong>State Violence:</strong> These exams are often administered by school nurses or doctors under state mandate, turning public health into surveillance.</li><li><strong>Gender Discrimination:</strong> No parallel exams exist for boys, reinforcing unequal sexual standards.</li><li><strong>Mental Health Trauma:</strong> Survivors report PTSD, depression, and suicidality due to the violation and its aftermath.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legal and Policy Reform</h3>
<ul><li>Support bans on virginity testing in school health protocols and national sexual health policies.</li><li>Promote consent-based health services for minors with oversight from youth-led advisory boards.</li></ul>
<h3>Medical Practice Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Require medical licensing boards to disqualify practitioners who conduct virginity tests.</li><li>Include anti-virginity testing training in medical education.</li></ul>
<h3>Curricular Change</h3>
<ul><li>Replace abstinence policing with evidence-based, comprehensive sexuality education.</li><li>Develop youth-led curricula that challenge purity culture and promote bodily autonomy.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Support Systems</h3>
<ul><li>Fund confidential counseling and reporting systems in schools for survivors of sexual health coercion.</li><li>Establish peer support networks in affected regions.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Virginity testing is not healthcare—it is institutionalized sexual violence. UNFPA must act to dismantle these discriminatory practices, transform school health systems, and affirm the right of all youth to dignity, privacy, and informed choice.</p>
