<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Digital political advertising has transformed election campaigns, enabling unprecedented reach but also facilitating opaque funding and manipulation. Current laws lag behind rapid digital innovation, allowing “dark ads” with minimal disclosure. This memo proposes strengthening real-time reporting, creating a public searchable digital ad library, holding platforms accountable, and supporting civil society monitoring. Federal-state coordination is critical for consistent enforcement. These reforms will increase transparency, reduce hidden influence, and restore public trust in elections.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Digital political ads constitute a growing share of campaign spending.</li><li>Platforms, such as Facebook and Google, have faced criticism for inadequate ad transparency.</li><li>Existing disclosure laws were designed before digital platforms’ dominance and are insufficiently enforced.</li><li>“Dark ads” target micro-segments with limited public visibility.</li><li>Some states have enacted stronger digital disclosure rules, but federal standards remain weak.</li><li>Civil society organizations actively monitor digital political ads but lack resources.</li><li>Coordination between federal and state agencies is limited.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Digital platforms enable hyper-targeted political messaging, but disclosure gaps prevent voters from understanding who funds and targets these ads. Real-time reporting would deter abuse by increasing accountability. Public ad libraries empower watchdogs and journalists. Holding platforms accountable incentivizes compliance and technological innovation for transparency. Supporting civil society improves independent oversight, especially of complex digital data. Harmonizing federal and state rules avoids regulatory fragmentation and enforcement loopholes.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Mandate Real-Time Reporting of All Digital Political Ads</h3>
<ul><li>Require submission of ad data (expenditure, targeting, duration, funding sources) within 24 hours to the FEC.</li><li>Standardize a platform-agnostic reporting format accessible to small and large campaigns.</li></ul>
<h3>Create and Maintain a Public, Searchable Digital Ad Library</h3>
<ul><li>Platforms must provide updated, searchable databases with advertiser identity, spending, reach, and targeting metadata.</li><li>Provide API access for researchers and watchdogs.</li></ul>
<h3>Hold Digital Platforms Accountable for Compliance</h3>
<ul><li>Impose enforceable penalties (fines, restrictions) for noncompliance.</li><li>Require proactive ad monitoring systems and independent audits.</li></ul>
<h3>Support Civil Society and Capacity Building</h3>
<ul><li>Fund NGOs and watchdogs for compliance monitoring and public education.</li><li>Develop training materials for youth and grassroots campaigns.</li></ul>
<h3>Coordinate Federal and State Efforts</h3>
<ul><li>Harmonize federal rules with stronger state laws.</li><li>Establish a federal-state enforcement task force.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Modernizing digital ad disclosure is essential for election integrity. These reforms will increase transparency, empower voters, and reduce dark money’s influence online. Federal leadership, combined with state cooperation and civil society support, can close existing loopholes and build a more trustworthy democratic process.</p>
