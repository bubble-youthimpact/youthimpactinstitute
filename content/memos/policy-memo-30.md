<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Indigenous youth are disproportionately incarcerated in many countries and face systems that erase their identity and disrupt their communities. This memo recommends that UNESCO promote culturally grounded, community-based justice approaches that support healing and self-determination for Indigenous youth.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>In countries, such as Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, Indigenous youth represent a shocking proportion of incarcerated minors—often 5 to 15 times their population share.<br>Root causes include:</p>
<ul><li>Intergenerational trauma from colonization and residential schools.</li><li>Discriminatory policing and lack of culturally appropriate legal representation.</li><li>Displacement from land, language, and ceremony.</li></ul>
<p>Prison further isolates Indigenous youth from their communities and traditions.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Key barriers include:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Cultural Erasure:</strong> Detention often prohibits spiritual practices and cultural contact.</li><li><strong>Systemic Racism:</strong> Courts and prisons apply harsher penalties to Indigenous youth.</li><li><strong>Colonial Justice Models:</strong> Incarceration ignores Indigenous concepts of justice and restoration.</li><li><strong>Lack of Data Sovereignty: </strong>Communities have little control over justice-related data.</li></ul>
<p>UNESCO must act to protect Indigenous cultural survival within justice systems.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Cultural Safety Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Require youth facilities to accommodate ceremony, language, and Indigenous spiritual leaders.</li><li>Train staff in Indigenous histories, rights, and trauma-informed care.</li></ul>
<h3>Alternative Justice Pathways</h3>
<ul><li>Promote community-led justice circles, land-based programs, and elder-led diversion efforts.</li><li>Support funding for Indigenous-led youth rehabilitation centers.</li></ul>
<h3>Policy Collaboration</h3>
<ul><li>Work with Indigenous nations to co-create international justice standards.</li><li>Respect Indigenous data governance and knowledge systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Visibility and Voice</h3>
<ul><li>Amplify Indigenous youth advocates at global justice and education forums.</li><li>Support storytelling, art, and language projects led by incarcerated Indigenous youth.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>True justice cannot exist without cultural survival. UNESCO must uphold Indigenous rights in every detention context and help build systems rooted in healing, not punishment.</p>
