<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Private prisons incentivize incarceration for profit, cut corners on safety and rehabilitation, and fuel systemic abuses. Although President Biden ended federal contracts with private prisons, they persist at the state level, where most incarceration occurs. This memo urges Democratic governors to adopt comprehensive state bans on private prisons and invest in public alternatives rooted in rehabilitation, labor rights, and community reintegration.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 100,000 people are held in private prisons in the U.S.</li><li>Private prison companies lobby for punitive policies and detention quotas.</li><li>Incarcerated people in private facilities face higher rates of violence and medical neglect.</li><li>States, such as California and Illinois, have enacted bans, proving feasibility.</li><li>Public prisons, while flawed, are more accountable to democratic oversight.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Private prisons profit from mass incarceration and perpetuate inhumane conditions. Their existence undermines public trust in justice institutions. By banning these facilities, Democrats can signal a clear departure from carceral profiteering and invest in alternatives that prioritize human dignity. States serve as critical battlegrounds for this reform, especially as federal leverage is limited.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>State-Level Bans and Contract Phaseouts</h3>
<ul><li>Pass legislation prohibiting new private prison contracts and sunsetting existing ones.</li><li>Ban out-of-state transfers to private facilities.</li></ul>
<h3>Public Alternatives and Oversight</h3>
<ul><li>Fund new public correctional programs focused on education, mental health, and reentry.</li><li>Create state-level prison ombudsman offices to monitor conditions and accountability.</li></ul>
<h3>Labor and Economic Transition Support</h3>
<ul><li>Provide retraining and job placement for affected private prison employees.</li><li>Invest in community economic development to replace prison-driven economies.</li></ul>
<h3>Public Awareness and Coalition Building</h3>
<ul><li>Partner with formerly incarcerated advocates to raise public awareness of private prison abuses.</li><li>Coordinate state party messaging around public safety, ethics, and cost transparency.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Banning private prisons is a defining opportunity for Democratic governors to align justice systems with public values. By rejecting profit-driven incarceration and investing in public alternatives, Democrats can demonstrate their commitment to decarceration, accountability, and human rights.</p>
