<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Predictive policing uses machine learning to forecast where crimes might occur or who is likely to commit them. Tools, such as PredPol and HunchLab, feed historical arrest data—already biased by over-policing of marginalized neighborhoods—into algorithms that “predict” risk zones or individuals. In Los Angeles and London, youth have been added to gang databases based on algorithmic suspicion alone. These tools often operate without public knowledge, due process, or appeal.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ol><li><strong>Feedback Loops:</strong> Biased data leads to biased predictions, reinforcing over-surveillance.</li><li><strong>Criminalization of Youth:</strong> Young people are flagged preemptively based on where they live or who they associate with.</li><li><strong>Opacity:</strong> Communities and even elected officials often don’t know these tools are in use.</li><li><strong>Erosion of Trust:</strong> AI policing destroys relationships between youth and public institutions.</li></ol>
<p>Predictive algorithms don’t predict crime—they predict police behavior. And they often target the wrong people.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>UNESCO should advocate for a ban on predictive policing involving minors.</li><li>Require human review and judicial oversight of any AI-informed youth surveillance.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate public disclosure of predictive policing tools and their data sources.</li><li>Establish youth legal aid clinics to challenge AI-generated suspicion.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Push for international standards limiting AI use in carceral and public safety contexts.</li><li>Require algorithmic impact assessments before adoption.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Fund youth-led community safety alternatives to algorithmic policing.</li><li>Support digital rights workshops on AI and criminal justice.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Youth safety should not come at the cost of youth freedom. UNESCO must oppose AI technologies that turn neighborhoods into digital prisons and reimagine public safety grounded in care, not code.</p>
