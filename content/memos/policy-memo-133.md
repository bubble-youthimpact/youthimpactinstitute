<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The proliferation of misinformation and political manipulation in digital advertising has eroded democratic discourse. This memo advocates for a modernized Fairness Doctrine to regulate online political ads, ensuring transparency, truthfulness, and balanced discourse on major platforms.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>The original Fairness Doctrine (1949–1987) required broadcasters to present contrasting views on controversial issues.</li><li>Digital platforms now function as dominant media gatekeepers without comparable regulation.</li><li>Disinformation in political ads disproportionately targets youth, marginalized voters, and non-English-speaking communities.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Reviving a fairness standard for political ads on digital platforms could mitigate polarization and manipulation.<br>Youth are especially vulnerable to algorithmic echo chambers and political disinformation.<br>A modernized doctrine could establish basic norms of digital civic fairness without infringing free speech.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Regulatory Action</h3>
<ul><li>Create a Digital Fairness Rule requiring platforms to provide political ad transparency reports.</li><li>Mandate a “right of reply” mechanism for misleading or one-sided paid political content.</li></ul>
<h3>Content Monitoring and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Develop a third-party fact-checking consortium for political ads with bipartisan oversight.</li><li>Create an appeals mechanism for candidates or organizations unfairly targeted by disinformation.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Focused Education</h3>
<ul><li>Launch a national digital media literacy curriculum in partnership with public schools.</li><li>Fund peer-led workshops on identifying and reporting misleading political ads.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Reinstating a digital-era Fairness Doctrine will ensure that online political discourse meets basic standards of truth and balance. With youth voters increasingly shaping election outcomes, this reform is vital to maintaining a healthy democracy.</p>
