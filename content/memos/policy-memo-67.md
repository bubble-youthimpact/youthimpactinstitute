<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Neurodivergent youth are overrepresented in juvenile justice systems worldwide due to behavioral misunderstandings, lack of community-based support, and systemic bias. This memo calls on the UNHRC to investigate and challenge the criminalization of neurodivergent youth by framing it as a rights violation. Structural reforms—including mandatory disability training for police and diversion programs rooted in neurodiversity awareness—are urgently needed.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Global patterns of discrimination against neurodivergent youth in legal systems include:</p>
<ul><li>Youth with autism, ADHD, and learning disabilities are up to 3 times more likely to be arrested or incarcerated.</li><li>Behavioral traits, such as  emotional dysregulation or difficulty following commands, are misread as aggression or defiance.</li><li>In many countries, disability is not systematically recorded in the justice process, hiding the true scale of the issue.</li><li>Legal representation often lacks neurodivergence training, undermining fair trials and informed testimony.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Current justice systems criminalize cognitive difference:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Behavior Misinterpretation:</strong> Sensory overload, stimming, or communication differences are seen as noncompliance or threat.</li><li><strong>Lack of Accommodations:</strong> Interrogations and court processes are not adapted for neurodivergent cognition or processing needs.</li><li><strong>School-to-Prison Pipeline:</strong> Neurodivergent students face higher rates of exclusion, leading to increased justice contact.</li><li><strong>Policing Without Training:</strong> Law enforcement lacks disability-informed de-escalation tools, increasing risk of harm.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Justice System Reform and Data Collection</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate disaggregated data on disability status in juvenile justice systems across member states.</li><li>Issue UNHRC guidance on protecting neurodivergent youth from discriminatory policing and detention.</li></ul>
<h3>Police and Legal Personnel Training</h3>
<ul><li>Require disability-focused de-escalation and communication training for all youth-serving officers.</li><li>Develop legal protocols to support neurodivergent witnesses, defendants, and complainants.</li></ul>
<h3>Community-Based Alternatives</h3>
<ul><li>Fund peer-led, trauma-informed diversion programs for neurodivergent youth.</li><li>Promote decriminalization of behaviors linked to sensory overload, shutdowns, or executive dysfunction.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>The incarceration of neurodivergent youth reflects deep injustice and systemic failure. By reframing this crisis as a human rights issue, the UNHRC can pressure governments to end criminalization and create legal systems that affirm the dignity and needs of all minds.</p>
