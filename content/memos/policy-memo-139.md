<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Climate change is increasingly driving global displacement, yet current U.S. asylum law excludes climate-related migration. This memo urges the DNC to support legislation recognizing “climate refugee” status under U.S. immigration law, ensuring the U.S. meets its humanitarian obligations and leads in a just transition. Such recognition would center environmental justice, youth solidarity, and international cooperation.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 21.5 million people are displaced annually due to climate-related disasters (UNHCR).</li><li>U.S. asylum law only recognizes persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or social group.</li><li>Climate migrants often face exploitation, statelessness, or deportation.</li><li>Youth climate activists globally are demanding recognition of climate-induced migration.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>The U.S. cannot ignore the human toll of the climate crisis—especially when it disproportionately affects the Global South and Indigenous communities. Recognizing climate refugees would reflect scientific and moral realities, repair international trust, and offer youth-led climate movements a tangible policy win. As climate events intensify, this status becomes not only ethical but pragmatic.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legal Recognition Framework</h3>
<ul><li>Introduce the Climate Refugee Protection Act to amend the Immigration and Nationality Act.</li><li>Define “climate refugee” as a person displaced due to environmental degradation, sea-level rise, or disasters.</li></ul>
<h3>Humanitarian Resettlement Pathways</h3>
<ul><li>Establish a new visa category and increase annual refugee caps for climate-displaced persons.</li><li>Prioritize applicants from climate-vulnerable nations and territories (e.g., Pacific Islands, Sahel, Bangladesh).</li></ul>
<h3>Global Youth Solidarity Networks</h3>
<ul><li>Support youth-led international climate migration advocacy coalitions.</li><li>Fund exchange fellowships for young climate leaders from affected regions.</li></ul>
<h3>Domestic Preparedness and Support</h3>
<ul><li>Ensure legal aid and housing support for climate migrants resettled in the U.S.</li><li>Train youth service corps to assist with refugee integration and cross-cultural community-building.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Climate displacement is a defining human rights issue of our time. By recognizing climate refugees, the DNC can align U.S. policy with youth values, international law, and ecological responsibility. Leadership on this issue will cement the party’s commitment to justice in a warming world.</p>
