<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Governments are deploying AI to flag families “at risk” of future child neglect, disproportionately surveilling poor, racialized, and disabled youth. These predictive child protection systems violate the right to family life and dignity. UNESCO must call for a ban on algorithmic family profiling.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Predictive risk models, such as Allegheny County’s AFST, claim to detect which families may abuse or neglect children, using data from housing, welfare, education, and justice systems. These tools disproportionately flag marginalized families for investigation or removal, often before any harm occurs. Youth experience forced separation, foster placement, and trauma—based on opaque statistical suspicion.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Digital Eugenics:</strong> Risk scores reflect poverty, not parental capacity.</li><li><strong>Intergenerational Punishment:</strong> Youth flagged due to parental history, not present behavior.</li><li><strong>Carceral Data Fusion:</strong> Welfare and justice databases create surveillance pipelines.</li><li><strong>Silencing of Youth:</strong> Affected children are rarely consulted in these decisions.</li></ul>
<p>AI cannot measure love, care, or resilience—but it can destroy families based on correlation.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>Declare AI risk scoring of family suitability a rights abuse.</li><li>Prohibit its use in child welfare investigations without direct cause.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate open-source disclosure of child risk algorithms.</li><li>Provide independent family advocates to challenge algorithm-driven investigations.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Require impact assessments showing no racial or economic bias before deployment.</li><li>Promote community-led social support over digital flagging.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Fund youth testimony projects on AI involvement in family separation.</li><li>Train youth ombudspersons to monitor algorithmic decisions in foster systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Predicting harm should never come at the expense of care. UNESCO must defend the rights of families from statistical dispossession and ensure that no child is made vulnerable by a machine pretending to protect.</p>
