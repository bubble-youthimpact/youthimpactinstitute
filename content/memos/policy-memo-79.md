<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>As digital services expand across public life, older adults face growing exclusion due to limited access and digital literacy. This memo urges the ITU to implement a global older adult digital inclusion initiative, with infrastructure, education, and age-friendly design as cornerstones.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 60% of people aged 65+ in low- and middle-income countries lack internet access.</li><li>Essential services—from banking to healthcare—are increasingly online-only.</li><li>Cognitive and physical accessibility issues are rarely considered in app or interface design.</li><li>Ageism in digital spaces perpetuates exclusion and self-censorship.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Older adults' digital exclusion is multidimensional:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Infrastructure Gap:</strong> Older adults in rural and low-income areas often lack broadband access.</li><li><strong>Design Inaccessibility:</strong> Interfaces assume digital fluency and overlook vision, motor, and memory changes.</li><li><strong>Digital Literacy Barriers:</strong> Training programs rarely target older users, particularly in non-urban areas.</li><li><strong>Data Privacy Risks:</strong> Seniors are often targeted by fraud or phishing, with limited recourse.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Infrastructure Expansion</h3>
<ul><li>Fund age-focused internet access programs with senior centers as digital hubs.</li><li>Partner with telecom companies for discounted elder connectivity plans.</li></ul>
<h3>User-Centered Design Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Develop age-friendly digital interface guidelines and universal design protocols.</li><li>Certify digital tools that meet accessibility standards for older users.</li></ul>
<h3>Targeted Education</h3>
<ul><li>Create public digital literacy curricula tailored to seniors.</li><li>Support intergenerational tech training programs.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Digital exclusion of older adults is an urgent equity issue. ITU can lead global efforts to ensure that longevity in the digital age means participation, not marginalization.</p>
