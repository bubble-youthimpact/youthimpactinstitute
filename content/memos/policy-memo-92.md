<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>With elders passing and younger generations disengaging from traditional faiths, vast repositories of oral religious knowledge risk disappearance. This memo calls for UNESCO to support youth-led digital documentation and intergenerational dialogue to preserve religious literacy across traditions.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>In rural, Indigenous, and diasporic communities, religion is passed orally through story, chant, ritual, and prayer.</li><li>Migration, secularization, war, and climate displacement disrupt these lineages.</li><li>Youth often feel alienated from ancestral religious forms due to language loss, colonial trauma, or perceived irrelevance.</li><li>Yet many are now using digital tools—TikTok, podcasts, oral history apps—to reconnect with sacred traditions on their terms.</li><li>Without intentional support, religious literacy—especially from marginalized traditions—will erode within a generation.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Heritage Loss:</strong> Sacred knowledge is treated as folklore, not as living epistemology, in heritage policies.</li><li><strong>Youth-Led Recovery:</strong> Young people want to document elders but lack funding, tech, or mentorship.</li><li><strong>Digital Divide:</strong> Low-income and displaced communities often lack tools to digitize oral heritage.</li><li><strong>Policy Neglect:</strong> UNESCO’s heritage criteria favor architecture and elite texts over oral and ritual-based traditions.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Funding Youth Documentation</h3>
<ul><li>Launch a global grant program for youth to record sacred stories, songs, and rituals in endangered traditions.</li><li>Provide equipment and training in ethical, collaborative oral history methods.</li></ul>
<h3>Intergenerational Platforms</h3>
<ul><li>Support multimedia archives that connect youth and elders in religious transmission.</li><li>Promote language-learning and sacred text translation projects led by young community members.</li></ul>
<h3>Policy Recognition</h3>
<ul><li>Expand Intangible Cultural Heritage listings to include oral religious practices.</li><li>Host global forums on sacred literacy, inviting youth archivists to present.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Religious literacy is not just about belief—it is about memory, identity, and continuity. UNESCO must invest in the sacred knowledge young people are trying to preserve before it disappears with the last storyteller or the last prayer in a native tongue.</p>
