<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Despite increasing recognition of bodily autonomy as a human right, intersex infants and youth continue to face non-consensual, medically unnecessary surgeries under the guise of “normalization.” These procedures violate international law and produce long-term physical and psychological harm. This memo calls on the UNHRC to issue a binding resolution condemning such practices, establish intersex protection monitoring mechanisms, and fund survivor-led policy development.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Intersex people are born with sex characteristics that do not fit typical binary definitions of male or female.<br>Invasive procedures to “correct” these variations continue globally:</p>
<ul><li>60+ countries permit early genital surgeries without consent under “medical necessity” (UN Free &amp; Equal, 2023).</li><li>Surgeries often occur before the age of two, with no legal requirement for deferral or child assent.</li><li>A 2022 intersex youth report showed that over 75% of respondents experienced PTSD or depression linked to early medical interventions.</li><li>Intersex advocates have long demanded a ban on surgeries until the individual can participate in decision-making.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>The persistence of forced surgeries reflects deeper systemic failures:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Medical Paternalism:</strong> Physicians are taught to “normalize” anatomy, prioritizing societal conformity over patient autonomy.</li><li><strong>Parental Pressure and Misinformation:</strong> Parents are often not given neutral counseling, leading to decisions made under duress.</li><li><strong>Legal Grey Zones:</strong> Most countries have no explicit laws banning these procedures, allowing human rights violations to occur with impunity.</li><li><strong>Data Scarcity:</strong> Intersex identities are poorly tracked in health systems, masking the scale of harm and impeding reform.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legal Safeguards and Monitoring</h3>
<ul><li>Support national bans on all non-consensual, non-emergency intersex surgeries.</li><li>Establish intersex rights reporting mechanisms within UNHRC’s Universal Periodic Review process.</li></ul>
<h3>Informed Parental and Medical Guidance</h3>
<ul><li>Develop UNHRC-supported counseling protocols that present affirming, non-coercive information to parents of intersex children.</li><li>Mandate ethics review boards for all proposed intersex medical interventions under age 12.</li></ul>
<h3>Intersex-Led Policy Design</h3>
<ul><li>Fund survivor-led commissions to shape laws and standards.</li><li>Support intersex youth organizing to ensure age-appropriate, peer-informed policy development.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Forced “corrective” surgeries on intersex youth are among the last forms of medically sanctioned bodily violation that still persist under silence. The UNHRC has the authority and responsibility to end this practice by embedding intersex autonomy into the global human rights framework. Protecting intersex youth is a litmus test for our commitment to dignity, diversity, and consent.</p>
