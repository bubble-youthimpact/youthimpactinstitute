<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Language barriers in juvenile detention systems lead to educational exclusion, legal misunderstanding, and isolation. This memo urges UNESCO to establish language rights protections for incarcerated youth and promote multilingual education and legal interpretation access in justice systems.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Incarcerated youth often come from migrant, Indigenous, or linguistic minority backgrounds.<br>When detained, they may:</p>
<ul><li>Not understand legal charges or proceedings.</li><li>Be placed in monolingual schooling or counseling.</li><li>Face stigma for speaking their native language.</li></ul>
<p>This violates their cultural and cognitive rights and increases recidivism risks.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Major barriers include:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Monolingual Systems:</strong> Most detention facilities operate only in the dominant national language.</li><li><strong>Interpreter Shortages:</strong> Legal systems lack trained juvenile interpreters.</li><li><strong>Punitive Language Policies:</strong> Some centers ban non-dominant languages as a “discipline” measure.</li><li><strong>Educational Gaps:</strong> Lack of mother-tongue education impedes learning and development.</li></ul>
<p>Language access is a justice issue, not just an educational one.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Policy Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Enshrine language rights protections for incarcerated youth in international legal education guidelines.</li><li>Require access to interpreters during legal, medical, and educational processes.</li></ul>
<h3>Multilingual Education</h3>
<ul><li>Fund bilingual and heritage language instruction in detention systems.</li><li>Promote language preservation projects for detained Indigenous youth.</li></ul>
<h3>Cultural Inclusion</h3>
<ul><li>Encourage staff training in cultural humility and multilingual support.</li><li>Support multilingual storytelling, theater, and translation programs.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitoring</h3>
<ul><li>Launch audits on linguistic equity in juvenile justice across member states.</li><li>Develop a multilingual rights compliance index.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Language is identity, access, and power. UNESCO must ensure that no child is silenced, misjudged, or denied opportunity because they speak a different tongue.</p>
