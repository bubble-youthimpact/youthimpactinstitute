<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>LGBTQ+ youth face disproportionate rates of incarceration and abuse behind bars. This memo calls for UNESCO to prioritize the protection of LGBTQ+ youth in detention systems and promote justice reforms that address identity-based criminalization, harassment, and isolation.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>In many countries, LGBTQ+ youth are criminalized for survival behaviors (e.g., sex work, homelessness) or targeted by biased policing.<br>Once incarcerated, they are often:<br>Denied gender-affirming care or clothing.<br>Placed in isolation “for protection,” amounting to de facto solitary confinement.<br>Subject to verbal and sexual harassment by staff or peers.<br>These experiences cause long-term psychological harm and increase suicide risk.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Core issues:<br>Overrepresentation: In the U.S., 40% of incarcerated girls identify as LGBTQ+.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Legal Gaps:</strong> Few countries have explicit protections for queer and trans youth in custody.</li><li><strong>Medical Neglect:</strong> Gender-affirming health care is often denied or pathologized.</li><li><strong>Lack of Safe Housing:</strong> Youth are often housed based on sex assigned at birth.</li></ul>
<p>UNESCO must position LGBTQ+ youth dignity and safety as central to justice reform.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Policy and Training</h3>
<ul><li>Promote international guidelines prohibiting discrimination in youth detention.</li><li>Train facility staff in LGBTQ+ rights, trauma-informed care, and inclusive language.</li></ul>
<h3>Health and Safety</h3>
<ul><li>Ensure access to gender-affirming care, mental health services, and peer support.</li><li>Ban “protective” isolation for LGBTQ+ youth and develop safe, affirming housing options.</li></ul>
<h3>Community-Based Alternatives</h3>
<ul><li>Fund diversion programs led by LGBTQ+ organizations.</li><li>Support decriminalization of survival behaviors linked to identity.</li></ul>
<h3>Visibility and Advocacy</h3>
<ul><li>Platform formerly incarcerated LGBTQ+ youth in UNESCO justice forums.</li><li>Support global data collection and research on LGBTQ+ youth incarceration.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>No one should be punished for who they are. UNESCO can lead a new global standard in protecting LGBTQ+ youth from criminalization and ensuring that all children are treated with dignity—inside and outside of the justice system.</p>
