<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The use of IQ thresholds in determining eligibility for disability benefits and special education services is discriminatory and outdated. These cutoffs exclude neurodivergent youth whose cognitive profiles don’t align with narrow metrics of “intelligence.” This memo urges the Social Security Administration and Department of Education to eliminate IQ-based eligibility requirements and adopt holistic, functional criteria.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>IQ-based eligibility remains entrenched in federal and state disability policy. The SSA and many school districts use a cutoff (typically IQ ≤70) to determine support access. Neurodivergent youth—such as those with autism, ADHD, or processing disorders—may be highly verbal or score well on tests but still face significant functional limitations. Racially biased IQ testing has been repeatedly challenged in court, especially due to its disproportionate denial of services to Black and Latinx youth.<br>Key concerns:</p>
<ul><li>IQ tests were not designed for disability classification.</li><li>They ignore adaptive functioning, emotional regulation, sensory needs, and executive challenges.</li><li>Youth with spiky cognitive profiles (common in autism or dyslexia) are often denied services despite real needs.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Relying on IQ tests entrenches a medical model of disability that privileges “deficits” over lived experience.</li><li>A rights-based approach demands support based on functional barriers, not flawed metrics.</li><li>Removing IQ thresholds would especially benefit racially marginalized, twice-exceptional, and underserved youth.</li><li>Advocates report that arbitrary IQ cutoffs result in prolonged legal battles and trauma for families.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Eliminate Federal IQ Thresholds in Policy</h3>
<ul><li>Remove all federal policies that condition benefits or services on fixed IQ scores.</li><li>Direct agencies to adopt functional assessments based on ability to perform daily life tasks, sensory/environmental needs, and communication.</li></ul>
<h3>Redefine Disability Eligibility Criteria</h3>
<ul><li>Require multidimensional evaluations that account for executive functioning, self-regulation, and sensory integration.</li><li>Center youth and family input in assessment processes.</li></ul>
<h3>Train Assessors and Administrators</h3>
<ul><li>Train disability assessors in neurodiversity-affirming approaches.</li><li>Fund public education campaigns to shift norms away from IQ-based categorization.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Access to disability rights should not depend on arbitrary test scores. Ending IQ cutoffs aligns policy with modern neuroscience and disability justice—ensuring youth are supported for who they are, not how they test.</p>
