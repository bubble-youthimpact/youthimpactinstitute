<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Climate change threatens disabled youth in disproportionate and often invisible ways—from inaccessible evacuation protocols to climate curricula that exclude disability perspectives. UNESCO must embed disability inclusion into its climate education and resilience programming globally.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Persons with disabilities are 2–4 times more likely to be injured or die during natural disasters. Yet disability is largely absent from disaster education, early warning systems, and school-based climate curricula. Disabled youth—especially in the Global South—are excluded from climate activism and policymaking. This invisibility reflects a broader gap in intersectional climate education.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Disaster Planning Gaps:</strong> School drills and evacuation routes rarely consider mobility, sensory, or cognitive needs.</li><li><strong>Curricular Erasure:</strong> Climate education rarely includes disabled perspectives or climate-related disability impacts.</li><li><strong>Youth Exclusion:</strong> Disabled youth are underrepresented in global youth climate networks and funding.</li><li><strong>Interagency Silos:</strong> Climate, disability, and education sectors lack collaboration.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Inclusive Climate Education</h3>
<ul><li>Fund disability-aware climate curricula co-designed with disabled students.</li><li>Promote intergenerational climate storytelling from disabled communities.</li></ul>
<h3>Disaster Resilience and Safety</h3>
<ul><li>Ensure all school disaster plans meet universal design standards.</li><li>Train students with disabilities as peer safety ambassadors.</li></ul>
<h3>Leadership and Participation</h3>
<ul><li>Include disabled youth in national and global youth climate delegations.</li><li>Fund climate leadership programs for disabled youth in frontline regions.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitoring and Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Integrate disability-specific indicators into UNESCO’s disaster resilience assessments.</li><li>Audit existing school safety plans for disability inclusion.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Disability inclusion is not ancillary to climate justice—it is central. UNESCO must act now to ensure disabled youth are safe, educated, and empowered as leaders in the face of global environmental crisis.</p>
