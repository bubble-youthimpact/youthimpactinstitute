<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Around the world, neurodivergent youth—especially those with ADHD and autism—are disproportionately prescribed psychotropic medications without adequate informed consent or access to alternative supports. This memo calls on WHO to address this crisis by issuing global prescribing guidelines, investing in non-pharmacological interventions, and mandating youth-informed consent protections.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Medical systems often pathologize neurodivergence and default to pharmacological control:</p>
<ul><li>Over 60% of neurodivergent youth in high-income countries are prescribed medication by age 12.</li><li>In low- and middle-income countries, medication is often the only available intervention, regardless of appropriateness.</li><li>Youth and families report coercive prescribing practices and inadequate explanation of risks.</li><li>Behavioral traits, such as stimming or hyperactivity, are pathologized rather than understood as valid expressions.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>This overmedication trend stems from structural neglect and systemic ableism:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Lack of Holistic Services:</strong> Therapy, peer support, and sensory integration services are underfunded or nonexistent.</li><li><strong>Medical Gatekeeping:</strong> Physicians often act as gatekeepers to school accommodations, incentivizing diagnosis and medication.</li><li><strong>Inadequate Consent:</strong> Youth are rarely given full explanations of side effects or alternatives.</li><li><strong>Global Inequity:</strong> Low-resource countries face drug donation programs without corresponding training or regulation.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Evidence-Based Prescribing Guidelines</h3>
<ul><li>WHO should develop global consensus standards on psychotropic prescribing for neurodivergent youth.</li><li>Require alternatives to be offered and documented before medication is prescribed.</li></ul>
<h3>Informed Consent and Oversight</h3>
<ul><li>Create youth-friendly informed consent protocols that include visual aids and choice-based formats.</li><li>Establish independent oversight boards to review prescribing patterns in pediatric psychiatry.</li></ul>
<h3>Non-Pharmacological Supports</h3>
<ul><li>Fund culturally relevant, community-based services including occupational therapy, sensory-friendly spaces, and peer groups.</li><li>Train providers to view neurodivergent behaviors through affirming, not punitive, lenses.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Medication should never be the only option. Neurodivergent youth deserve care systems that prioritize consent, dignity, and holistic wellbeing. WHO has the reach and authority to set a new global standard—one that centers young people, not just symptoms.</p>
