<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>During and after the COVID-19 pandemic, online education surged—but left many students with disabilities behind. UNESCO should spearhead a global standard for accessible online learning platforms, requiring closed captioning, screen reader compatibility, and universal design in digital education.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Remote learning exposed digital inequalities for students with disabilities: inaccessible interfaces, lack of assistive tech, and poor teacher preparedness. Deaf, blind, neurodivergent, and mobility-impaired students were particularly excluded. The shift to hybrid learning models now demands long-term accessibility policies that have so far been lacking in global education tech initiatives.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Tech Design Exclusion:</strong> Edtech platforms often ignore WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines).</li><li><strong>Educator Training Gaps:</strong> Teachers struggle to adapt content for diverse learning needs online.</li><li><strong>Language and Locality:</strong> Global South platforms rarely offer accessibility in multiple languages.</li><li><strong>Disability Data Void:</strong> Online accessibility is rarely tracked in education evaluations.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Global Accessibility Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Require all UNESCO-supported edtech tools to meet WCAG 2.1 standards.</li><li>Promote multilingual AI captioning tools and text-to-speech features.</li></ul>
<h3>Educator Empowerment</h3>
<ul><li>Fund accessibility-focused digital pedagogy training for teachers.</li><li>Develop open-source accessible curricula co-designed with disability advocates.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth and Disability Participation</h3>
<ul><li>Support disabled youth in evaluating and redesigning education tech.</li><li>Create fellowships for students with disabilities to lead accessibility innovation.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitoring and Equity Metrics</h3>
<ul><li>Embed accessibility indicators in digital education monitoring frameworks.</li><li>Promote disaggregated data collection on disability and online learning outcomes.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>If education is moving online, accessibility must come first. UNESCO can ensure that students with disabilities are not excluded from the digital future of learning, by making inclusive design the global standard—not the exception.</p>
