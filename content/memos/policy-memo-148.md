<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Although Americans can vote at 18, they cannot run for Congress until 25 (House) or 30 (Senate). These age minimums exclude millions of politically engaged young adults from full democratic participation. Amending the Constitution to lower these thresholds would align eligibility with voting rights and ensure generational representation in policymaking.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>The age limits for federal office were set in the 18th century, before widespread youth enfranchisement.</p>
<ul><li>Today’s youth are highly informed and politically active but barred from running for national office.</li><li>At the local and state levels, elected officials under 25 are increasingly common.</li><li>Congress shapes policies that disproportionately affect youth—climate, education, labor—but lacks generational diversity.</li><li>Age thresholds disproportionately impact young leaders from working-class and marginalized communities.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Modern neuroscience confirms cognitive maturity for political reasoning develops by late adolescence.</li><li>Other countries (e.g., Canada, the UK, France) allow youth under 25 to run for national office.</li><li>Critics argue life experience matters, but voters—not outdated rules—should decide qualifications.</li><li>Lowering eligibility expands civic imagination and disrupts the gerontocratic status quo in Congress.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Constitutional Amendment</h3>
<p>Propose and ratify a 29th Amendment to lower eligibility to</p>
<ul><li>House of Representatives: 18</li><li>Senate: 21</li></ul>
<p></p>
<h3>State Ratification Campaigns</h3>
<ul><li>Mobilize youth-led state-level coalitions to pressure legislatures for ratification.</li></ul>
<h3>Congressional Youth Fellowship Pipeline</h3>
<ul><li>Create paid pathways from high school and college organizing into legislative service.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>In a democracy, representation should not be age-gated. Lowering the age of eligibility honors the political capacity of youth and expands democracy to those already entrusted to vote, serve, and lead.</p>
