<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Legacy admissions—preferences for applicants related to alumni—entrench generational privilege and block access for first-generation, low-income, and BIPOC students. This memo calls on Congress to ban legacy preferences at any college or university receiving federal funds, leveling the playing field for all applicants.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Legacy admissions began in the early 20th century to preserve elite institutions as spaces for wealthy white families.</p>
<ul><li>Up to 75% of top U.S. colleges consider legacy status in admissions.</li><li>Legacy applicants are 3–5 times more likely to be admitted than non-legacy peers.</li><li>First-generation, low-income, and underrepresented students are disadvantaged by legacy bias.</li><li>Federal dollars (e.g., research grants, Pell funding) sustain these institutions despite inequitable access.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Legacy admissions function as hereditary privilege, not merit-based opportunity.</li><li>Banning legacy preferences is more popular than banning affirmative action.</li><li>Some elite universities (e.g., Johns Hopkins, Amherst) have successfully eliminated legacy admissions with no negative impact.</li><li>Federal intervention is justified when public funds subsidize exclusion.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Funding Conditions</h3>
<ul><li>Amend the Higher Education Act to prohibit legacy admissions at federally funded institutions.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Enforcement</h3>
<ul><li>Require annual public reporting of admissions data disaggregated by legacy status.</li></ul>
<h3>Support for First-Gen Students</h3>
<ul><li>Expand federal grants for institutions serving high numbers of first-generation applicants.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Ending legacy admissions is a necessary step toward higher education equity. No student should be admitted—or excluded—based on the accidents of ancestry. Withholding federal dollars from legacy-biased institutions affirms that public funding must serve public good.</p>
