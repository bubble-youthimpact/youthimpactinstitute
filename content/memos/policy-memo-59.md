<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>In criminalizing youth engaged in survival sex, many states are failing to protect LGBTQ+ youth from exploitation, instead punishing them for structural vulnerability. This memo urges UNODC to adopt a harm-reduction, rights-based approach to survival sex among queer youth and push states to replace punitive frameworks with supportive interventions.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>LGBTQ+ youth are overrepresented in the sex trade globally due to homelessness, poverty, and family rejection. Rather than offering protection, many legal systems criminalize sex work broadly—even for minors—subjecting queer youth to incarceration, police violence, and social services withdrawal. This drives exploitation further underground and removes pathways to justice.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Criminalization and Stigma:</strong> Police harassment and moral panic make it impossible for youth to seek safety or report abuse.</li><li><strong>Lack of Alternatives:</strong> Emergency shelters, school reentry programs, and employment pathways are often closed to queer youth labeled “criminals.”</li><li><strong>Human Trafficking Framework Confusion:</strong> Law conflates all youth sex work with trafficking, ignoring agency and survival context.</li><li><strong>Incarceration Risks:</strong> Queer youth in detention face sexual violence, isolation, and conversion attempts.</li><li><strong>Exploitation by Systems:</strong> Social workers and officers sometimes exploit youth under threat of arrest or “rehabilitation.”</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legal Reform</h3>
<ul><li>Support the decriminalization of survival sex by minors and removal of “status offenses” from penal codes.</li><li>Develop anti-trafficking frameworks that distinguish between coercion and survival behavior.</li></ul>
<h3>Service Expansion</h3>
<ul><li>Fund housing, mental health care, and employment programs designed by and for LGBTQ+ youth in survival sex.</li><li>Mandate non-judgmental support services for youth outside the criminal legal system.</li></ul>
<h3>Training and Oversight</h3>
<ul><li>Train law enforcement and social workers on harm reduction and trauma-informed care.</li><li>Create independent watchdog mechanisms to monitor abuse against LGBTQ+ youth in custody.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Led Alternatives</h3>
<ul><li>Invest in queer youth cooperatives and creative economies to provide safe, affirming income alternatives.</li><li>Support peer-led outreach programs in urban centers.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Criminalizing queer youth for surviving is not protection—it is abandonment. UNODC must lead in promoting structural reforms that prioritize dignity, safety, and support over punishment and shame.</p>
