<p>b]Executive Summary<br>Linking college food programs with local food systems enhances food quality, affordability, and sustainability while supporting community economies. This memo proposes USDA-facilitated partnerships between colleges and local farmers, food cooperatives, and nonprofits to develop farm-to-campus initiatives. Such collaborations address food insecurity by increasing access to fresh, nutritious food and fostering local economic development.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>College food service programs often rely on processed and non-local foods due to cost and logistics.</li><li>Local food systems can provide fresher, culturally relevant, and affordable options.</li><li>Farm-to-school programs demonstrate success but remain underutilized in higher education.</li><li>Building these partnerships requires coordination and funding support.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Facilitating local food partnerships can:</p>
<ul><li>Improve nutrition and student satisfaction with campus food.</li><li>Support local agriculture and food justice initiatives.</li><li>Enhance sustainability and reduce food waste.</li><li>Overcome logistical and financial challenges through coordinated federal support.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>USDA should:</p>
<ul><li>Provide grants to colleges for developing farm-to-campus programs.</li><li>Facilitate networks connecting educational institutions with local producers.</li><li>Offer technical assistance on supply chain management and food safety.</li><li>Encourage integration of nutrition education focused on local foods.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Supporting partnerships between colleges and local food systems is a strategic approach to improve student nutrition and combat food insecurity. USDA’s facilitation and funding will strengthen community resilience and student well-being.</p>
