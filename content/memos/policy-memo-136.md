<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The dual crises of youth unemployment and climate change demand a bold, integrated response. A federally funded Civilian Climate Corps (CCC) would create green jobs for young people, build climate resilience in vulnerable communities, and promote civic stewardship. This memo proposes that the DNC lead the call for a CCC revival as a cornerstone of its climate and economic agenda, with youth leadership and equity as central pillars.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>The original Civilian Conservation Corps (1933–1942) employed 3 million Americans.</li><li>Youth unemployment remains high, especially among BIPOC and rural populations.</li><li>Climate disasters disproportionately affect poor and marginalized communities.</li><li>The idea has bipartisan precedent and growing public support, particularly among young voters.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>A modern CCC would create thousands of entry-level jobs in renewable energy, sustainable agriculture, disaster preparedness, and public land restoration—sectors often inaccessible to youth. It would also reframe climate action as a civic duty and public good, giving young people tangible roles in building a sustainable future. This policy aligns economic justice with ecological repair, offering a unifying narrative for the DNC.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Federal Legislation Blueprint</h3>
<ul><li>Champion a Green New Deal for Youth Act authorizing $10B+ in CCC funding.</li><li>Ensure placements in conservation, energy efficiency, housing resilience, and water infrastructure.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Leadership and Equity Provisions</h3>
<ul><li>Set minimum targets (40%) for youth from frontline, tribal, and low-income communities.</li><li>Fund mentorship ladders to train youth as local green project coordinators.</li></ul>
<h3>Public-Private Partnership Model</h3>
<ul><li>Incentivize state and local governments to co-fund CCC expansions through matching grants.</li><li>Create pipelines into union apprenticeships and technical education in clean industries.</li></ul>
<h3>Visibility and Civic Framing</h3>
<ul><li>Rebrand CCC participants as “Climate Stewards” with public service recognition and loan forgiveness eligibility.</li><li>Launch media campaigns to highlight CCC contributions in each congressional district.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>A Civilian Climate Corps is not just a climate program—it’s a nation-building investment in youth and justice. It would provide purpose, training, and security while advancing the DNC’s vision for a green, inclusive economy. Reviving this New Deal legacy can energize young voters and deliver real results in struggling communities.</p>
