<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Religious beliefs deeply shape youth sexual health knowledge, yet many global programs ignore or dismiss faith perspectives. This memo calls on UNFPA to integrate rights-based, interfaith dialogue into youth sexual and reproductive health initiatives.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Religious norms often influence family planning, menstruation stigma, and youth sexuality.</li><li>Youth in faith-based communities frequently lack accurate, nonjudgmental information.</li><li>Sexual health programs that exclude religious frameworks face local backlash or underuse.</li><li>Faith leaders can be powerful allies or obstacles—depending on how programs are framed.</li><li>Intersectional challenges are acute for LGBTQ+ youth in conservative faith spaces.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Polarized Frames:</strong> Youth are often forced to choose between religious identity and bodily autonomy.</li><li><strong>Faith-Literate Gaps:</strong> Health workers often lack tools to navigate religious worldviews.</li><li><strong>Youth Silencing:</strong> Young people of faith are excluded from shaping sexual rights discourse.</li><li><strong>NGO Disconnect:</strong> Secular health agencies often fail to engage trusted community institutions.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Interfaith Training and Dialogue</h3>
<ul><li>Develop culturally adapted SRHR materials in collaboration with progressive faith leaders.</li><li>Train health workers to respectfully engage religious values while upholding rights.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Leadership</h3>
<ul><li>Fund youth-of-faith peer educators to lead inclusive reproductive justice efforts.</li><li>Create interfaith SRHR councils within national youth strategies.</li></ul>
<h3>Community Mobilization</h3>
<ul><li>Partner with religious institutions to reduce stigma around sexuality and contraception.</li><li>Ensure LGBTQ+ youth in religious settings receive affirming health services.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Faith and sexual health rights are not mutually exclusive. UNFPA must recognize youth-of-faith as key partners in creating inclusive, culturally grounded SRHR programs that respect both belief and autonomy.</p>
