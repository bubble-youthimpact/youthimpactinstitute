<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Foreign investment in U.S. farmland and freshwater assets poses threats to national sovereignty, food security, and rural livelihoods. This memo recommends a total ban on foreign ownership of U.S. agricultural land and critical water infrastructure—an initiative with rising bipartisan and youth support. The DNC should lead the effort by framing the issue around democratic control, Indigenous sovereignty, and ecological justice.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 40 million acres of U.S. farmland are foreign-owned (2021 USDA data).</li><li>Countries, such as China and Saudi Arabia, have invested heavily in U.S. food and water assets.</li><li>Water rights purchases in the West risk long-term monopolization.</li><li>Farmers, tribal nations, and youth climate groups oppose extractive land practices.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Foreign-controlled land and water create vulnerabilities in domestic food production and infrastructure. These acquisitions often displace small farmers, restrict Indigenous access, and degrade ecosystems. Young voters increasingly see land justice and ecological self-determination as core political issues. A bold stance from the DNC would align with economic populism and environmental protection, uniting progressive and rural constituencies.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Legislative Framework</h3>
<ul><li>Support a federal bill banning future foreign ownership of farmland, aquifers, and irrigation systems.</li><li>Require full disclosure of current foreign landholders and establish a federal review board.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth and Indigenous Inclusion</h3>
<ul><li>Create a federal Land Sovereignty Fund to support Indigenous land reclamation and Black farmer cooperatives.</li><li>Prioritize young farmer access to reclaimed land through affordable leases and training grants.</li></ul>
<h3>Public Transparency Measures</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate public maps and databases of foreign land holdings and water use permits.</li><li>Launch a "Protect Our Land" campaign led by rural youth and tribal youth networks.</li></ul>
<h3>Climate and Food Security Strategy</h3>
<ul><li>Tie land policy to climate resilience, sustainable agriculture, and anti-monopoly enforcement.</li><li>Fund climate corps land stewards to support regenerative practices on newly protected lands.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Land is power. By banning foreign ownership of farmland and water, the DNC can champion democratic control over essential resources, appeal to diverse constituencies, and show that national security includes ecological and generational sovereignty. It’s time to put U.S. land back in the hands of those who live on it.</p>
