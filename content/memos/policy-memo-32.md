<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Across the world, incarcerated youth are forced to work in prison industries with little or no pay. This memo recommends that UNESCO work with member states to end forced labor in youth detention systems, promote fair labor protections, and ensure that rehabilitation does not come at the cost of exploitation.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>In the name of vocational training or facility maintenance, many juvenile facilities require incarcerated youth to work in dangerous or demeaning jobs.<br>Problems include:</p>
<ul><li>Unpaid labor for government or private contracts.</li><li>Retaliation or discipline for refusing to work.</li><li>Absence of workplace protections or education value.</li></ul>
<p>This practice violates multiple international child labor conventions.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Why it persists:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Legal Loopholes:</strong> Incarceration is often used to justify exemption from labor laws.</li><li><strong>Economic Incentives:</strong> Governments and corporations benefit from free youth labor.</li><li><strong>Lack of Oversight:</strong> Few mechanisms exist to ensure fair conditions or consent.</li><li><strong>Stigma:</strong> Society often ignores abuse in prisons under a “they deserve it” mentality.</li></ul>
<p>Prison labor should never replace education or serve as punishment.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Global Prohibition Framework</h3>
<ul><li>Advocate for a global ban on unpaid or coerced labor in youth facilities.</li><li>Ensure alignment with ILO and CRC child labor standards.</li></ul>
<h3>Vocational Justice</h3>
<ul><li>Support vocational programs that include education, certification, and real wages.</li><li>Fund alternatives that build digital and creative skills, not manual servitude.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitoring and Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Require transparent labor audits in detention centers.</li><li>Develop a certification for ethical youth rehabilitation programs.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Advocacy</h3>
<ul><li>Support youth organizing and storytelling on prison labor.</li><li>Platform formerly incarcerated youth in labor and justice forums.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Forced labor is not rehabilitation—it is exploitation. UNESCO must stand firm against carceral labor abuses and lead the movement for dignity-based rehabilitation for all youth.</p>
