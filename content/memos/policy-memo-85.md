<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>State secularism, while vital for pluralism, is increasingly weaponized to suppress youth religious expression. This memo urges the UNHRC to monitor and reform secular governance models that infringe upon students' rights to religious identity and practice in public spaces.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Globally, youth face school-based prohibitions on religious dress, prayer, or dietary adherence.</li><li>State neutrality is often interpreted through dominant religious norms, marginalizing minority faiths.</li><li>In France, India, and parts of Central Asia, hijabs, turbans, and kippahs have been banned in schools.</li><li>Youth who cannot access state schooling due to religious discrimination suffer long-term educational and economic impacts.</li><li>International human rights law protects both freedom of religion and freedom from religion—but implementation is uneven.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Selective Neutrality: Secular laws often codify majority norms while restricting minority visibility.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Gender and Religious Suppression:</strong> Young women are disproportionately targeted by dress-code bans.</li><li><strong>Civic Alienation:</strong> Restrictive policies contribute to youth distrust in democratic institutions.</li><li><strong>Lack of Accountability:</strong> Few national monitoring bodies track violations of religious expression rights in schools.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>UNHRC Monitoring and Reporting</h3>
<ul><li>Launch a special rapporteur review of youth religious freedom in state institutions.</li><li>Create a global report card on religion-related school exclusions.</li></ul>
<h3>Legislative Guidance</h3>
<ul><li>Issue model secularism frameworks that ensure equal religious expression rights.</li><li>Encourage states to balance anti-discrimination and freedom of conscience obligations.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Engagement</h3>
<ul><li>Fund interfaith youth councils to advise on education and secularism policies.</li><li>Protect the right to wear religious symbols and observe holy days in all state schools.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Secularism must not become a tool for erasing youth religious identity. The UNHRC must act to protect the right of young people to express their faith in public education without fear of exclusion or punishment.</p>
