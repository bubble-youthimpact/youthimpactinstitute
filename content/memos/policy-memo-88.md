<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Faith-based economic models—like Islamic finance, Christian cooperatives, and Buddhist giving—offer alternative approaches to poverty, inequality, and ecological care. This memo urges UNDP to incorporate these sacred economic systems into a sustainable development strategy.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Religious frameworks have long governed community finance: zakat, waqf, tithes, mutual aid, and temple economies.</li><li>These systems prioritize redistribution, dignity, and collective care over profit.</li><li>Youth-led movements are reviving faith-based models for housing, debt relief, and mutual support.</li><li>Yet, global development ignores or co-opts sacred economies, privileging neoliberal metrics.</li><li>Sacred economies also challenge extractive and growth-driven paradigms, aligning with post-growth goals.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Epistemic Bias:</strong> Economic policies often marginalize religious approaches as “irrational.”</li><li><strong>Youth Exclusion:</strong> Young faith-based entrepreneurs lack access to capital and networks.</li><li><strong>NGO Misalignment:</strong> Development agencies fail to understand zakat or waqf potential in poverty alleviation.</li><li><strong>Post-Colonial Opportunity:</strong> Sacred economies represent indigenous resistance to colonial-capitalist paradigms.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Faith-Inclusive Development Models</h3>
<ul><li>Recognize religious economic practices as legitimate poverty interventions.</li><li>Map and support youth-led sacred economic innovation (e.g., shariah-compliant cooperatives).</li></ul>
<h3>Resource Mobilization</h3>
<ul><li>Integrate zakat and church giving into national SDG funding strategies.</li><li>Partner with interfaith mutual aid networks to distribute social safety nets.</li></ul>
<h3>Policy Innovation</h3>
<ul><li>Fund research on sacred economic models aligned with degrowth, care, and climate justice.</li><li>Host global dialogues on religion, youth, and post-capitalist futures.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>UNDP must treat faith not as a barrier but as a wellspring of economic imagination. Sacred economies deserve space in the global search for systems that nourish life rather than extract it.</p>
