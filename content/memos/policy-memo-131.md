<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The Electoral College continues to distort democratic representation by allowing presidential candidates to win office without the popular vote. This memo explores how the Democratic Party can support the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact (NPVIC) to phase out the Electoral College without a constitutional amendment. Strategically advancing the NPVIC offers a constitutionally viable path to electoral equity and youth engagement.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Two of the last five U.S. presidents won without winning the popular vote.</li><li>Youth voters are disproportionately represented in large urban centers, which are often devalued by the Electoral College system.</li><li>The NPVIC binds participating states to allocate their electors to the national popular vote winner once states totaling 270 electoral votes have joined.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Eliminating the Electoral College resonates with younger voters seeking a more direct democracy.</li><li>Supporting the NPVIC sidesteps the arduous constitutional amendment process.</li><li>The DNC’s endorsement could incentivize additional states to join, building legitimacy for the compact.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Coalition Building</h3>
<ul><li>Partner with state legislatures and governors in swing and blue states to accelerate NPVIC adoption.</li><li>Establish a national youth-led campaign to pressure holdout legislatures to join the compact.</li></ul>
<h3>Legal and Policy Advocacy</h3>
<ul><li>Fund legal research and public education campaigns on the constitutionality of the NPVIC.</li><li>Develop model legislation and testimony kits for state hearings.</li></ul>
<h3>Public Engagement and Messaging</h3>
<ul><li>Frame Electoral College reform as a racial, generational, and urban equity issue.</li><li>Highlight youth disenfranchisement in battleground states to galvanize student-led movements.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Ending the Electoral College through interstate compacts offers a viable, democratic route to electoral reform. By aligning with this youth-driven demand, the Democratic Party can demonstrate its commitment to majority rule and long-term structural change.</p>
