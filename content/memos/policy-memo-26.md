<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Harsh school discipline policies contribute to rising youth incarceration rates, especially among marginalized communities. This memo recommends that UNESCO promote restorative justice practices in schools to dismantle the school-to-prison pipeline and support student mental health, safety, and learning.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Zero-tolerance policies in schools—such as suspensions for minor infractions—disproportionately affect Black, Indigenous, and disabled youth. These policies increase student contact with law enforcement and elevate dropout and incarceration risks.<br>For example:</p>
<ul><li>Students suspended even once are three times more likely to enter the juvenile justice system.</li><li>In the U.S., Black students represent 15% of enrollment but 33% of school-based arrests.</li><li>Similar patterns are observed in Brazil, South Africa, and parts of Europe.</li></ul>
<p>These exclusionary approaches undermine UNESCO’s education-for-all mission.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Systemic problems include:<br>Over-Policing: Increased presence of school resource officers (SROs) leads to criminalization of behavioral issues.</p>
<ul><li><strong>Implicit Bias:</strong> Discipline is often applied disproportionately to marginalized students.</li><li><strong>Lack of Support Services:</strong> Schools often lack counselors or trauma-informed staff.</li><li><strong>Punitive Culture:</strong> Students are treated as threats rather than learners.</li></ul>
<p>Restorative justice offers a constructive, relational alternative grounded in empathy and accountability.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>UNESCO should:</p>
<h3>Restorative Education Models</h3>
<ul><li>Promote school-wide restorative justice programs, including peer mediation and conflict circles.</li><li>Fund research and dissemination of best practices in diverse contexts.</li></ul>
<h3>Disciplinary Reform Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Develop international guidelines for equitable school discipline.</li><li>Encourage member states to track and publish disaggregated discipline data.</li></ul>
<h3>Training and Capacity Building</h3>
<ul><li>Provide teacher training in de-escalation, trauma response, and bias awareness.</li><li>Support hiring of school-based mental health professionals and social workers.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Led Transformation</h3>
<ul><li>Involve students in designing restorative discipline policies.</li><li>Support youth advocacy for education equity and justice.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>School should be a place of healing, not a gateway to incarceration. UNESCO can transform global education by shifting from punishment to restoration—and ensuring all youth feel seen, safe, and supported in their learning environments.</p>
