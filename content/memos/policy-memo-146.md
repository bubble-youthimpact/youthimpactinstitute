<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) is a proven tool for poverty reduction, yet many young workers—especially students and part-time employees—are excluded due to age or income restrictions. Expanding the EITC to include youth under 25 without dependents would improve financial stability, reduce student debt, and incentivize workforce participation. This memo proposes eligibility reforms that center youth equity in anti-poverty efforts.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>The EITC is one of the most effective anti-poverty programs in the U.S., but current rules exclude workers under 25 without children, even if they work and pay taxes. This leaves millions of young low-income Americans—particularly students and gig workers—without vital support.</p>
<ul><li>Over 17 million young adults (ages 18–24) are employed but ineligible for the EITC.</li><li>Youth without children remain excluded, even when living independently and attending school.</li><li>Expanding EITC would reduce racial and educational inequities in income support.</li><li>Past proposals, including the American Rescue Plan’s temporary expansion, showed strong bipartisan potential.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li>Targeted EITC expansion boosts youth economic mobility.</li><li>Helps working students afford education and basic living expenses.</li><li>Could reduce reliance on predatory loans and credit debt.</li><li>Supports low-wage youth workers without disincentivizing full-time school.</li><li>Cost-effective investment in early economic empowerment.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Amend EITC Eligibility Requirements</h3>
<ul><li>Lower age of eligibility to 18 for childless filers, especially those enrolled in school or training.</li><li>Remove full-time student exclusions.</li></ul>
<h3>Phase-In Benefit for Part-Time Work</h3>
<ul><li>Introduce a modified phase-in schedule for part-time youth workers to ensure inclusion.</li><li>Allow coordination with Pell Grants and SNAP without penalty.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Focused Outreach</h3>
<ul><li>Fund IRS and community-based tax preparation programs tailored to young workers.</li><li>Partner with colleges, community centers, and job corps to maximize uptake.</li></ul>
<h3>Monitor Long-Term Outcomes</h3>
<ul><li>Study the effects of youth EITC expansion on graduation rates, housing stability, and entrepreneurship.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Young workers deserve the same access to earned income support as older adults. Expanding the EITC to include students and youth workers is a commonsense, bipartisan strategy to invest in future taxpayers, reduce inequality, and give young people a financial foundation for independence.</p>
