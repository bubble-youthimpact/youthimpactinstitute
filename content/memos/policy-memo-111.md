<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Across the United States, children and their families are saddled with thousands of dollars in juvenile court fees—ranging from electronic monitoring costs to restitution fines. These practices criminalize poverty, push families into debt, and increase recidivism. This memo proposes that the DNC adopt a national platform calling for the full abolition of all fines and fees in juvenile justice. This reform aligns with the party’s values on racial equity, child welfare, and ending criminalization of poverty.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>48 states currently impose some form of financial penalties on youth or their families.</li><li>Court costs, supervision fees, and electronic monitoring charges can exceed $2,000 per case.</li><li>Low-income and BIPOC families are disproportionately targeted and impacted.</li><li>Evidence shows that fines increase recidivism and disrupt youth-family bonds.</li><li>States, such as California and New Jersey, have already abolished juvenile fees with bipartisan support.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Juvenile fines do not serve public safety or rehabilitation—they serve as regressive revenue mechanisms at the expense of the most marginalized. Eliminating them is a clear way for Democrats to lead on youth justice reform while reducing wealth-based punishment. The issue offers moral clarity and strong youth, religious, and civil rights coalition support.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Federal Incentives and Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Make federal juvenile justice funding contingent on fee abolition.</li><li>Establish DOJ guidelines condemning all financial penalties for youth offenses.</li></ul>
<h3>Debt Relief and Record Expungement</h3>
<ul><li>Support retroactive forgiveness of juvenile justice debts.</li><li>Tie debt forgiveness to automatic expungement of associated records.</li></ul>
<h3>Public Education and Coalition Building</h3>
<ul><li>Fund public campaigns about the harms of youth fines and fees.</li><li>Build multi-state organizing coalitions to support local repeal campaigns.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Juvenile fines and fees are a quiet engine of economic injustice in the legal system. Eliminating them represents a straightforward, values-driven step toward a more equitable youth justice system. By taking up this cause, the Democratic Party can unite voters around a tangible and bipartisan reform that directly improves young lives.</p>
