<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>The influx of undisclosed “dark money” and the rise of digital political advertising have eroded public trust in the American electoral system. Current campaign finance laws are outdated and ill-equipped to regulate online spending, leaving voters in the dark about who is influencing elections. This memo recommends a comprehensive update to federal campaign finance laws that enhances transparency, limits undisclosed funding, and addresses digital ad disclosures. Strengthening these laws will restore faith in democratic processes and empower voters with the information necessary to make informed decisions.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>The Supreme Court’s Citizens United (2010) and subsequent rulings deregulated corporate and union spending, fueling dark money flows.</li><li>Digital political advertising now accounts for a significant portion of campaign spending but remains lightly regulated.</li><li>There are limited federal requirements for disclosure of donors behind super PACs and nonprofit “social welfare” groups.</li><li>Public trust in elections and political institutions has declined, partly due to perceived influence of shadowy funding.</li><li>Youth voters, while energized, express frustration with opaque funding that undermines their electoral engagement.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Existing campaign finance frameworks focus largely on traditional media and direct candidate contributions, failing to address modern digital realities. The lack of transparency in dark money funding creates asymmetries in political influence, allowing wealthy interests to shape narratives without accountability. Furthermore, digital platforms’ inconsistent ad policies exacerbate misinformation risks and limit voter awareness of funding sources. Without legal reform, these challenges will deepen political polarization and disillusionment among younger voters. Legislative updates must balance free speech protections with the imperative of transparency and electoral integrity.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Modernizing Campaign Finance Laws</h3>
<ul><li>Update disclosure requirements to include all digital political advertising expenditures, with real-time public reporting.</li><li>Mandate transparency for donors to nonprofit entities involved in electioneering communications.</li><li>Establish federal limits on contributions to super PACs and related dark money groups.</li></ul>
<h3>Strengthening Enforcement</h3>
<ul><li>Increase funding and authority for the Federal Election Commission (FEC) to investigate violations and enforce penalties.</li><li>Create a digital ad verification bureau within the FEC to audit online political ads.</li></ul>
<h3>Voter Education and Engagement</h3>
<ul><li>Fund public awareness campaigns explaining campaign finance and the importance of transparency.</li><li>Support youth-led watchdog organizations to monitor and report on campaign finance violations.</li></ul>
<h3>Public Funding Expansion</h3>
<ul><li>Expand access to public financing options for candidates to reduce dependency on large donors.</li><li>Promote matching funds programs that incentivize small-dollar contributions.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Modernizing campaign finance laws is critical to restoring voter trust and safeguarding democratic legitimacy in the digital age. The current regulatory framework lags behind technological and political developments, enabling dark money to influence elections unchecked. By enacting these reforms, the Democratic Party can lead the fight for cleaner, more transparent elections that resonate with younger voters and reinforce democratic accountability. These measures will not only improve electoral integrity but also empower citizens with the transparency needed to participate fully in the democratic process.</p>
