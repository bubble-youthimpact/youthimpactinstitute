<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Across Europe, Asia, and Latin America, hundreds of thousands of children with disabilities are placed in residential institutions—isolated from families, communities, and education. UNESCO must advocate for the end of institutionalization and support community-based, inclusive alternatives in line with the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>UNICEF estimates over 2.7 million children live in institutions worldwide—many placed solely due to disability. These settings often involve neglect, abuse, and lifelong exclusion. Despite international commitments, funding often prioritizes institutions over community-based services. This practice is a violation of children's right to family life, inclusive education, and community participation.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Policy Inertia:</strong> Many countries lack transition plans for deinstitutionalization.</li><li><strong>Funding Misdirection:</strong> International aid continues to support institutional infrastructure.</li><li><strong>Social Isolation:</strong> Institutionalized youth are denied meaningful peer interaction and civic participation.</li></ul>
<p>Youth Voice Absence: Those most affected are rarely consulted in reform processes.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Deinstitutionalization Mandates</h3>
<ul><li>Push for international moratoria on new residential institutions for children.</li><li>Require national deinstitutionalization roadmaps under SDG 4 and CRC reviews.</li></ul>
<h3>Community-Based Alternatives</h3>
<ul><li>Fund community-based inclusive education, respite care, and family support.</li><li>Train local disability advocates to support reintegration.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Led Reform</h3>
<ul><li>Establish youth advisory councils of former institutionalized children.</li><li>Create platforms for youth with disabilities to co-design policy solutions.</li></ul>
<h3>International Accountability</h3>
<ul><li>Track institutionalization trends in UNESCO data systems.</li><li>Prohibit international education aid from funding residential institutions.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Institutions are not homes. UNESCO can be a global leader in protecting the right of every child to grow up in a family, learn in a community, and thrive in society—regardless of disability.</p>
