<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>School resource officers (SROs) have become a normalized presence in K–12 schools, despite little evidence they improve safety. Instead, their presence fuels the school-to-prison pipeline, especially for Black, Latinx, disabled, and LGBTQ+ youth. This memo advocates for the removal of police from educational settings and the redirection of funds toward restorative justice, mental health, and community support staff. A reimagined approach to school safety is central to any youth-centered Democratic criminal justice agenda.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 14,000 officers are assigned to schools nationwide.</li><li>Black students are over three times more likely to be arrested at school than white students.</li><li>SROs often criminalize normal adolescent behavior, especially in marginalized communities.</li><li>Presence of police increases youth anxiety, truancy, and academic disengagement.</li><li>Districts that removed SROs saw no uptick in violence but did see increased student support access.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Police in schools do not prevent shootings but do perpetuate a culture of surveillance and punishment. Replacing SROs with trained professionals—such as counselors, social workers, and peer mediators—better supports youth safety, wellbeing, and inclusion. Democratizing school discipline and prioritizing care over control reflects party values on racial equity, education access, and youth mental health.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Divest from School Policing</h3>
<ul><li>Propose amendments to federal education funding that disqualify SRO funding in favor of youth support personnel.</li><li>Remove school policing from school safety grant eligibility.</li></ul>
<h3>Fund Restorative Justice and Mental Health</h3>
<ul><li>Create new funding streams for peer mediation, conflict resolution, and trauma recovery programs.</li><li>Support national training standards for youth-focused restorative justice practitioners.</li></ul>
<h3>Empower Community-Led Safety Models</h3>
<ul><li>Fund local youth councils to design school safety policies.</li><li>Encourage culturally competent alternatives developed in partnership with students and families.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Policing has no place in spaces of learning. The DNC must support a transformation of school safety policies that affirm dignity, prevent harm, and uplift the full humanity of young people—especially those historically criminalized. Investing in student-centered support systems is an investment in a more just and inclusive society.</p>
