<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Campus food pantries have become essential lifelines for food-insecure students, yet they are chronically underfunded and unevenly distributed. This memo proposes that federal higher education grant programs incorporate dedicated funding for establishing, expanding, and sustaining campus food pantries. Strengthening this infrastructure directly supports student well-being and retention, especially among marginalized populations.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 650 campus food pantries operate nationwide, serving millions of students.</li><li>Many pantries rely on limited donations and volunteer labor, leading to inconsistent hours and supplies.</li><li>Food pantries help reduce hunger but cannot meet demand without sustainable funding.</li><li>Federal higher education grants currently do not prioritize food security infrastructure.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Embedding pantry funding in federal grants will:</p>
<ul><li>Enable campuses to provide reliable, nutritious food access.</li><li>Foster partnerships with local food banks and nonprofits to expand reach.</li><li>Address gaps in rural and community colleges often overlooked by philanthropy.</li><li>However, pantries alone cannot solve food insecurity without complementary policy changes.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>Department of Education should:</p>
<ul><li>Include food security infrastructure as a criterion for Title III and Title V grant eligibility.</li><li>Allocate competitive grants for pantry startup and operational costs.</li><li>Encourage data collection on pantry usage to guide policy and funding.</li><li>Promote collaboration between pantries, counseling centers, and financial aid offices for holistic support.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Integrating food pantry funding into federal higher education grants creates sustainable campus resources essential to addressing food insecurity. This approach empowers institutions to meet students’ basic needs, fostering equitable academic success.</p>
