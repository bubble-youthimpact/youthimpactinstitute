<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Youth homelessness in the United States is rising—especially among LGBTQ+, foster care alumni, and undocumented youth. Despite Democratic leadership's rhetorical commitment to housing justice, policy responses remain underfunded, reactive, and siloed. This memo outlines a federal approach to youth homelessness that centers housing as a human right and embeds prevention in education, healthcare, and justice systems. The DCCC should champion this vision in its 2026 platform to demonstrate moral leadership and policy innovation.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Over 4.2 million youth experience homelessness annually in the U.S. (HUD, 2023).</li><li>Black, Indigenous, and LGBTQ+ youth are disproportionately affected.</li><li>Many fall through service gaps when aging out of foster care or leaving juvenile detention.</li><li>Current federal funding streams prioritize short-term shelters over long-term solutions.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Ending youth homelessness requires more than housing—it demands a shift in how public systems interact with marginalized youth. Education, foster care, and health systems must coordinate to prevent youth from entering the homelessness pipeline. A rights-based housing framework offers the Democrats an opportunity to realign with their values and reach disenfranchised young voters.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Prevention Infrastructure</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate school-based housing stability counselors in all Title I districts.</li><li>Fund exit-transition programs for foster care and juvenile justice-involved youth.</li></ul>
<h3>Housing First Expansion</h3>
<ul><li>Scale youth-specific Housing First programs with wraparound mental health services.</li><li>Provide rental assistance for youth-led households, including sibling caregivers.</li></ul>
<h3>Policy Integration and Federal Leadership</h3>
<ul><li>Establish a White House Office on Youth Homelessness Prevention.</li><li>Require interagency reporting on youth housing insecurity across HHS, HUD, and DOE.</li></ul>
<h3>Community-Led Solutions</h3>
<ul><li>Fund youth-run shelter cooperatives and housing trusts.</li><li>Support peer navigator programs to build trust and access among unhoused youth.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Youth homelessness is not inevitable—it’s the result of political choices. By embedding housing justice into its legislative agenda, the Democratic Party can protect its most vulnerable constituents and showcase bold leadership. This is both a moral and strategic imperative.</p>
