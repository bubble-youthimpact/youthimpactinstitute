<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Austerity-driven cuts to pensions, healthcare, and social care have devastated older populations globally. This memo calls on the IMF to reform loan conditions and economic advisory protocols that incentivize slashing elder-focused services, especially in the Global South.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>IMF programs often require public sector wage freezes and cuts to healthcare or pension systems.</li><li>Older adults rely more heavily on public infrastructure due to fixed incomes and health needs.</li><li>In Greece, Ecuador, and Tunisia, IMF-driven austerity has led to spikes in elder poverty and mortality.</li><li>Social protection floors for older adults are rarely upheld during structural adjustment programs.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>IMF economic conditionalities disproportionately harm older adults:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Healthcare Cuts:</strong> Lead to unmedicated chronic illness and institutional neglect.</li><li><strong>Pension Freezes:</strong> Leave aging populations unable to afford housing, food, or utilities.</li><li><strong>Privatization:</strong> Shifts costs onto elders who can’t navigate or afford market solutions.</li><li><strong>Gendered Impact:</strong> Older women, who have lower savings and pensions, suffer most.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>Human Rights-Based Lending</p>
<ul><li>Include age and health impact assessments in all IMF adjustment agreements.</li><li>Reject loan programs that cut basic elder entitlements.</li></ul>
<h3>Safeguard Social Protection</h3>
<ul><li>Establish non-negotiable floors for pensions, elder healthcare, and long-term care.</li><li>Incentivize governments to invest in age-friendly social infrastructure.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Consultation</h3>
<ul><li>Include elder advocacy groups in loan negotiation processes.</li><li>Publish elder-specific analyses of all adjustment impacts.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>The IMF must recognize older people as economic actors, not burdens. Austerity is a political choice, not an economic inevitability—and older adults must be protected from its most devastating consequences.</p>
