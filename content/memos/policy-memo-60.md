<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Global sexual health curricula rarely recognize asexual and aromantic identities, reinforcing narrow norms of sexual development. This memo calls on WHO to integrate ace and aro perspectives into sexual and reproductive health (SRH) materials, trainings, and youth services—ensuring inclusivity and mental health equity.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Asexual (experiencing little or no sexual attraction) and aromantic (experiencing little or no romantic attraction) youth face invisibility and stigma in health education and services. Many are misdiagnosed, pathologized, or pressured into relationships or sexual activity to meet normative expectations. Lack of recognition undermines bodily autonomy, mental wellbeing, and access to care.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Erasure in Curricula:</strong> SRH often treats attraction and sex as universal milestones, leaving ace and aro youth confused or excluded.</li><li><strong>Misdiagnosis and Conversion:</strong> Youth report being sent to therapy or subjected to hormonal treatments to "fix" their orientation.</li><li><strong>Medical Gatekeeping:</strong> Without identity recognition, ace and aro youth face barriers in accessing contraception, STI care, or counseling.</li><li><strong>Mental Health Harms:</strong> Many report depression, anxiety, or internalized shame due to misunderstanding from peers, professionals, and families.</li><li><strong>Cultural Diversity:</strong> In some cultures, non-sexual identity is more socially acceptable—but still misunderstood by health systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Curricular Inclusion</h3>
<ul><li>Revise WHO youth SRH frameworks to include ace and aro identities, diverse attraction spectrums, and voluntary celibacy.</li><li>Include ace/aro youth in developing new global health education modules.</li></ul>
<h3>Provider Training</h3>
<ul><li>Train SRH providers to recognize and affirm ace and aro orientations without pathologization.</li><li>Develop screening questions and counseling approaches that avoid assumptions about desire.</li></ul>
<h3>Mental Health Support</h3>
<ul><li>Fund identity-affirming counseling programs for ace/aro youth, especially in schools and universities.</li><li>Launch awareness campaigns to challenge myths and stigma.</li></ul>
<h3>Research and Data</h3>
<ul><li>Support global studies on ace/aro youth health outcomes and service barriers.</li><li>Disaggregate SRH data by orientation beyond binary LGBT+ categories.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Every youth has the right to health—whether or not they experience attraction. WHO must lead in expanding the definition of sexual wellbeing to include asexual and aromantic identities, affirming that health is about choice, not conformity.</p>
