<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Extreme heat disproportionately threatens older adults, who account for the vast majority of heat-related deaths. This memo calls on UNEP to integrate age-specific protections into urban climate adaptation plans, with special attention to cooling access, mobility, and disaster planning.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>By 2050, 70% of the world’s older population will live in cities, many in low-income or informal housing.</li><li>Older adults are physiologically more vulnerable to heat stress and air pollution.</li><li>Many cities lack heat action plans that address older people’s needs (e.g., mobility issues, isolation).</li><li>Cooling infrastructure (e.g., shade, water access) is often concentrated in wealthier districts.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Urban climate policies currently fail older adults by:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Overlooking Access:</strong> Heat warnings are often digital, inaccessible to those without phones or literacy.</li><li><strong>Neglecting Isolation:</strong> Older people living alone may not receive community check-ins or transportation help.</li><li><strong>Designing Inaccessibly:</strong> Cooling shelters may lack elevators, ramps, or adequate toilets.</li><li><strong>Underfunding Long-Term Change:</strong> Short-term responses ignore the need for structural urban redesign for aging bodies.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Cooling and Resilience Infrastructure</h3>
<ul><li>Require shaded walkways, water stations, and green roofs in city planning.</li><li>Prioritize cooling access in elderly care facilities and public housing.</li></ul>
<h3>Inclusive Communication and Mobility</h3>
<ul><li>Develop analog-friendly alert systems (e.g., radio, door-to-door health workers).</li><li>Provide accessible transit to cooling centers.</li></ul>
<h3>Climate-Aging Urban Metrics</h3>
<ul><li>Create “heat vulnerability maps” that include age as a key metric.</li><li>Fund city-based research centers on climate and aging.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>UNEP must ensure that climate adaptation is not youth-centered by default. Aging urban populations require intentional design and justice-driven responses to survive the heatwaves of tomorrow.</p>
