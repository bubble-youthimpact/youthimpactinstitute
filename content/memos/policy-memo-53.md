<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Despite increasing legal protections in some regions, LGBTQ+ youth continue to face workplace discrimination worldwide—from hiring bias to on-the-job harassment. This memo calls on the ILO to expand anti-discrimination frameworks, strengthen complaint mechanisms, and promote youth-led labor organizing in queer communities.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Workplace discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity contributes to high rates of unemployment, mental health distress, and economic instability among LGBTQ+ youth. These disparities are especially acute for transgender and nonbinary individuals. While the ILO has addressed gender and disability-based discrimination in past conventions, LGBTQ+ issues remain under-prioritized in global labor discourse.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Informal Sector Exploitation: Queer youth are overrepresented in informal, precarious work—often unregulated and without protections.<br>Hiring Discrimination: In countries with no legal protections, applicants are rejected based on appearance, name changes, or outing on social media.<br>Gendered Harassment: Trans youth often report misgendering, bathroom bans, and forced dress codes, with no recourse.<br>Policy Gaps: Only 40% of ILO member states ban SOGI-based employment discrimination. Enforcement is weaker still.<br>Youth Underrepresentation: LGBTQ+ labor issues are rarely represented in union leadership or workplace policy design.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Global Standards</h3>
<ul><li>Issue a formal ILO convention recognizing SOGI-based workplace discrimination as a labor rights violation.</li><li>Include queer youth in Decent Work country profiles and labor statistics.</li></ul>
<h3>Enforcement Tools</h3>
<ul><li>Create a complaints mechanism for SOGI labor discrimination, with anonymity protections.</li><li>Offer technical support to labor ministries to track SOGI-based violations.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Leadership</h3>
<ul><li>Fund youth-led queer worker cooperatives and unions.</li><li>Support leadership pipelines for LGBTQ+ individuals in labor organizing bodies.</li></ul>
<h3>Private Sector Engagement</h3>
<ul><li>Develop an ILO corporate equality index for multinational companies.</li><li>Incentivize inclusive workplace certifications in youth-heavy industries.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Queer youth should not have to choose between identity and income. The ILO must treat workplace dignity as indivisible from sexual and gender justice—elevating LGBTQ+ youth as not just workers, but as leaders of tomorrow’s labor movements.</p>
