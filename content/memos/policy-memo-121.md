<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>To effectively address food insecurity, colleges must systematically assess student needs and publicly report on food access challenges. This memo advocates for federal legislation mandating food security assessments as part of institutional reporting requirements tied to federal funding. Transparent data will improve accountability, inform policy, and drive resource allocation.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<ul><li>Food insecurity is often underreported due to stigma and lack of data infrastructure.</li><li>Few colleges conduct regular, comprehensive assessments of student food security.</li><li>Federal funding mechanisms require institutions to report on various student success metrics, but food security is not yet included.</li><li>Accurate data is essential for targeted interventions and funding justification.</li></ul>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<p>Mandating food security reporting will:</p>
<ul><li>Increase institutional awareness and prioritization of hunger issues.</li><li>Provide baseline data to tailor campus programs and policy initiatives.</li><li>Support longitudinal research on food insecurity’s impact on student outcomes.</li><li>However, institutions may require funding and technical assistance to implement assessments effectively.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<p>Congress should:</p>
<ul><li>Pass legislation requiring federally funded colleges to conduct annual food security surveys.</li><li>Include food security metrics in accreditation and federal funding eligibility reviews.</li><li>Provide grants to support survey implementation and data analysis.</li><li>Encourage transparency by requiring public reporting of aggregated data.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Legislating food security assessments and reporting is foundational to understanding and combatting student hunger. Congressional action will promote accountability and catalyze targeted policy responses nationwide.</p>
