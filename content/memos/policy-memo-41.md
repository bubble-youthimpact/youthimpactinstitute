<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Scholarship programs increasingly use AI to sort and evaluate applicants at scale. While efficient, these systems often encode socioeconomic, linguistic, and regional bias—excluding disadvantaged youth. UNESCO must establish ethical oversight to prevent algorithmic injustice in education access.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Automated scholarship scoring platforms analyze essays, grades, and behavioral data to rank applicants. But training data reflects existing privilege—favoring urban, English-speaking, test-prepared students. Rural, first-gen, disabled, and non-Western students often see their potential devalued by proxies for merit, like standardized grammar or facial affect. Without oversight, these systems silently reproduce educational exclusion under a veneer of objectivity.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Bias Amplification:</strong> Training on past winners bakes privilege into selection.</li><li><strong>Lack of Transparency:</strong> Applicants don’t know why they were rejected—or even that AI was used.</li><li><strong>Data Colonialism:</strong> Youth from the Global South are judged by Global North models of success.</li><li><strong>Disempowerment:</strong> Automated rejections demoralize youth without recourse or feedback.</li></ul>
<p>Algorithmic gatekeeping in scholarships risks building a new, invisible form of educational apartheid.</p>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Rights-Based AI Moderation</h3>
<ul><li>UNESCO should define education AI as a protected rights domain.</li><li>Require human review of all final scholarship decisions.</li></ul>
<h3>Transparency and Redress</h3>
<ul><li>Mandate disclosure if AI is used and allow applicants to request human reconsideration.</li><li>Require plain-language explanations for automated rejections.</li></ul>
<h3>Platform Regulation</h3>
<ul><li>Push for inclusive, multilingual training data and cultural validation of scoring criteria.</li><li>Prohibit use of AI in high-stakes youth decisions without fairness audits.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth Voice and Resilience</h3>
<ul><li>Fund youth-led audits of edtech and scholarship platforms.</li><li>Support platforms that use participatory scoring or peer-based evaluation models.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>Access to education should not depend on opaque algorithms built without youth input. UNESCO can ensure that AI in scholarship systems supports equity—not silently replaces it with discrimination dressed as efficiency.</p>
