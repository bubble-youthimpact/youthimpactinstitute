<h3>Executive Summary</h3>
<p>Queer refugee youth face disproportionately high risks of sexual violence, trafficking, and coercion in displacement settings. Despite UNHCR commitments to inclusion, many shelters and camps remain unsafe or inaccessible for LGBTQ+ youth. This memo urges the agency to implement specialized protection protocols and expand safe, affirming services for displaced queer youth globally.</p>
<h3>Background</h3>
<p>Across refugee contexts—from Syrian border camps to urban settlements in Central America—LGBTQ+ youth are often excluded from formal protection systems. They face abuse by peers, staff, and host communities. Without legal documents or family support, many engage in survival sex or are trafficked by organized crime. Lack of affirming shelters, healthcare, and asylum pathways compounds trauma.</p>
<h3>Analysis</h3>
<ul><li><strong>Invisible in Policy:</strong> Few refugee response plans include specific protections for LGBTQ+ youth, treating their needs as “special cases” or omitting them entirely.</li><li><strong>Staff Discrimination:</strong> Camp workers often lack training or harbor biases, making reporting unsafe.</li><li><strong>Lack of Segregated Shelters:</strong> Many LGBTQ+ youth avoid general shelters due to harassment, but separate housing is rarely available.</li><li><strong>Legal Limbo:</strong> Trans youth may be denied gender-affirming IDs or medical care, limiting access to services.</li><li><strong>Double Marginalization:</strong> LGBTQ+ youth who are also disabled, racial minorities, or sex workers face compound risks.</li></ul>
<h3>Recommendations</h3>
<h3>Protection Protocols</h3>
<ul><li>Develop specialized protection pathways and referral systems for LGBTQ+ youth in camp settings.</li><li>Train protection officers in SOGI sensitivity and youth mental health.</li></ul>
<h3>Safe Housing</h3>
<ul><li>Fund pilot safe houses within or near refugee camps for queer youth survivors of violence.</li><li>Ensure all shelters include anti-harassment mechanisms and anonymous reporting.</li></ul>
<h3>Legal and Health Access</h3>
<ul><li>Offer legal support for name/gender marker changes and asylum filings on SOGI grounds.</li><li>Integrate gender-affirming and trauma-informed care into refugee health systems.</li></ul>
<h3>Youth-Led Monitoring</h3>
<ul><li>Create peer support groups and advisory boards of displaced LGBTQ+ youth.</li><li>Document human rights abuses and report data disaggregated by SOGI and age.</li></ul>
<h3>Conclusion</h3>
<p>LGBTQ+ refugee youth are not just victims of conflict—they are victims of structural neglect. UNHCR must lead in recognizing their specific needs, defending their dignity, and funding protection programs rooted in lived experience and human rights.</p>
